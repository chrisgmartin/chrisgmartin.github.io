✓ Check yourself

Answer from memory before opening each one.

  1. You release crypto the moment a Pix deposit confirms. Why is that mostly safe, and what risk remains?

    Show answer

    Pix is a push rail with scheme-level finality, so the payer cannot pull the money back as with a card. The residual risk is the MED, the special return mechanism for fraud claims, which has a narrow window but is real. Release on confirmation and monitor MED claims. See fastest finality and the reversibility axis.

  2. Marketing wants card funding for EU crypto purchases because conversion is better. How do you set the release posture?

    Show answer

    Cards sit at the top of the reversibility scale, with chargebacks open for around 120 days. Pair 3DS2, ID matched to the card name, velocity limits and a delayed crypto release, or offer SCT Inst, which is final. Conversion gains do not cover losses on assets you cannot recall. See reversibility axis.

  3. Why can a USD to BRL payout cost 2 to 10 bps through a stablecoin and local Pix leg, against 50 bps or more through SWIFT?

    Show answer

    The on-chain leg replaces the chain of correspondent banks, each of which takes a cut and adds days. Settlement falls from days to minutes. It needs a licensed local partner for the Pix payout. See cheapest cross-border.

  4. You sell a monthly subscription in the EU, UK and US. Compare the refund exposure on SEPA Direct Debit, a UK variable recurring payment and US ACH debit.

    Show answer

    SEPA Direct Debit allows an unconditional refund for 8 weeks. A UK variable recurring payment is a push on a final rail, so there is no refund right. US consumer ACH debit allows a 60-day return. The first is the riskiest for a crypto top-up. See recurring and mandates.

  5. A treasury team must settle $25M with a US OTC desk the same day. Which rail, and why not RTP?

    Show answer

    Fedwire. It has no cap and gross settlement is irrevocable, at a cost of roughly $15 to $40. RTP is final and cheap, but the cap in the table is $10M per payment. See high-value B2B.

Takeaway

  • Pick a rail by what must be final before you release the asset, not by speed alone.
  • Instant push rails such as UPI, Pix, FedNow, RTP, SCT Inst and FPS give scheme-level finality but keep fraud-recall paths like Pix MED and UK APP reimbursement.
  • Pull rails carry reversal windows: about 120 days for cards, 8 weeks for SEPA Direct Debit, 60 days for consumer ACH, and an open-ended indemnity for BACS.
  • A stablecoin leg plus a local instant rail beats SWIFT on cost and time for LATAM payouts, but needs a licensed local partner.
  • High-value transfers belong on RTGS rails such as Fedwire, CHAPS and T2, because instant rails have per-payment caps.
  • Refunds go back the way the money came; cards, UPI and Pix are cleanest, and wires are the worst.
  • Treat reversibility as a spectrum and set the crypto release hold from it.
