Data Platform · Deep Dive

Building an Operational Ontology

How a data platform gives every machine, order, shipment, site and incident one identity across systems that disagree, keeps the truth fresh, carries provenance and permissions through every join, and serves it to people and agents. Deep dive, with GridDP as the running case.

If you only have an hour

Read 01 for what an operational ontology is and is not, then 02 (identity) and 05 (permissions that survive joins). Those three are the shape of the job; the rest is how to keep it true and serve it.

Section A · Orient
00 Start Here What this guide covers, the GridDP case extended to a first-party fleet, and the three framings to carry. 01 What an Operational Ontology Is Objects, properties, links and actions over live systems; how it differs from a knowledge graph, a semantic layer, a data warehouse and a CMDB; when a company needs one.
Section B · Model the world
02 Entity Identity and Resolution One identity for a machine that twelve systems name differently: keys, crosswalks, probabilistic matching, survivorship, and what to do when the match is wrong. 03 Change Capture and Freshness Cutting the time from something happening to the platform knowing it: log-based CDC, event time against processing time, documents and conversations as change streams, freshness as a measured number. 04 Provenance, History and Evidence Every fact keeps its source row, document span, time and transformation; bitemporal history; the model that makes 'show me why' a query.
Section C · Guard it
05 Permissions That Survive Joins Relationship-based access control over the object graph, source restrictions enforced in search and in derived answers, and what happens when an answer combines three systems with three policies. 06 Deletions, Corrections and Replay Propagating an erasure, a permission change or a corrected record through every derived table, index, cache and cited answer; replaying history without resurrecting what was removed.
Section D · Serve it
07 Retrieval for People and Agents Query and retrieval services that check the caller's permissions before data reaches a model, answer with citations, freshness and gaps, and let a new source be added without a new service. 08 Plan Versus Observation Turning the graph into business signals: comparing schedules, orders and budgets with what telemetry, tickets and shipments say happened, and surfacing the gaps that matter.
Section E · Operate it
09 Operating Guarantees Freshness, quality and security as SLOs with error budgets; tracing a wrong or stale answer to its cause and making that failure detectable next time. 10 The Interview Walkthrough The GridDP ontology designed end to end in forty minutes: what to draw, which numbers to say, the trade-offs to name, and the questions to ask back.

About this guide. A deep dive for data platform engineers who are asked to build the internal system of record for a company whose operations live in a dozen tools, so that people and agents can act on fresh, traceable, permissioned facts. Written in November 2026 with primary sources throughout; GridDP figures are illustrative teaching fixtures.
