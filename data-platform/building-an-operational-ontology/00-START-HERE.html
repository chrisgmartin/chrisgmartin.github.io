Scope & audience

This guide is for a data platform engineer who is handed a problem that sounds like integration and turns out to be epistemology: a company runs on a dozen systems that each describe the same machine, order, shipment, site or incident in their own words, and the people and agents who must act need one account of what is true, how fresh it is, where it came from, and who may see it. The deliverable is usually called an operational ontology. The eleven chapters build one for GridDP, the site's GPU-marketplace case study, from identity and change capture through provenance, permissions, deletion, retrieval for agents and the operating guarantees, and end with a forty-minute interview walkthrough.

It assumes the curriculum's ground: warehouses, CDC, orchestration, data tests. It does not re-teach them. Where a neighbouring guide owns a subject (lineage in ML Data Architecture, tenancy in retrieval in the RAG deep dive, agent permissions in Securing LLM Applications), the chapter links it and moves on.

GridDP, extended

The systems-design guide defines GridDP as a two-sided GPU-rental marketplace with a telemetry firehose, a live price market and money flowing both ways. This guide adds one fact, canon here only: GridDP now also builds and operates a first-party Reserved fleet. The additions below are illustrative teaching fixtures, like the rest of GridDP.

Three framings to carry

  1. An ontology is a warehouse with four extra obligations. Identity across systems, freshness as a measured number, provenance on every fact, and permissions that survive every join. Drop any one and the result is a reporting layer, which is fine, and is not what an operations team or an agent can act on. Chapter 01 makes the distinction precise.
  2. The unit of work is the fact, not the table. A fact is a value with a source, an observed time, a valid time, a transformation and a confidence. Everything hard in this guide, from erasure to citations to "what did we know on 14 October", becomes tractable once facts carry that envelope and impossible once they do not.
  3. Agents raise the stakes without changing the rules. An agent that answers from the ontology must see exactly what its caller may see, cite what it used, and say what is missing. That is the same contract a dashboard owes a human, enforced where it was previously assumed. Chapter 05 and chapter 07 carry it.

How this guide is sourced

Every external claim cites a primary source: the Palantir Foundry documentation for the product sense of the word, the Zanzibar paper and the OpenFGA documentation for relationship-based access, PROV-O for provenance, Debezium and the Dataflow Model for change capture, Fellegi and Sunter and Splink for record linkage, GDPR Article 17 for erasure, the SRE Workbook for SLOs, and the Apache Iceberg specification for history and deletes. Versions and figures are as of November 2026. GridDP's numbers are fixtures and say so.
