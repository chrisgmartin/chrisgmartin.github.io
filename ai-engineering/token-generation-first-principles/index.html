Token Generation from First Principles

How a decoder-only language model turns text into tokens and tokens into the next token, with the arithmetic: tokenizers and their cost, embeddings and position, attention one head at a time, the block and the forward pass, logits to a sampled token, the generation loop and its cache, scale and reasoning models, how to read a model card, and why models fail the way they do.

Section A · Orient
00 Start Here What this guide explains and in what order, the arithmetic you will do, the Harbourline prompt used throughout, and the vocabulary.
Section B · Inside the model
01 From Text to Tokens Byte-pair encoding and its relatives, vocabulary sizes, why numbers, names and other languages tokenize badly and cost more, special tokens and chat templates, and Harbourline's prompt counted in tokens. 02 Embeddings and Position Token embeddings and the residual stream, absolute positions to rotary embeddings, context extension, and why long contexts degrade. 03 Attention, One Head at a Time Queries, keys and values on a worked example, the causal mask, multi-head, multi-query and grouped-query attention, the quadratic cost, and what attention heads turn out to compute. 04 The Block and the Forward Pass The feed-forward network, normalisation and residuals, stacking layers, where eight billion parameters come from, FLOPs per token, and mixture-of-experts.
Section C · Generation
05 From Logits to a Token Softmax and temperature, top-k, top-p and min-p, repetition penalties, greedy and beam search, logprobs, why outputs are not deterministic, and the settings for Harbourline's extraction and chat. 06 The Generation Loop Autoregression, prefill and decode, why the KV cache exists and what it costs, stop conditions and streaming, and the latency arithmetic.
Section D · Scale and practice
07 Scale and Reasoning Models Encoder-decoder to decoder-only, scaling laws, the base, instruct and reasoning pipeline, test-time compute and thinking budgets, multimodal inputs as tokens, and what changed between 2017 and 2026. 08 Reading a Model Card The configuration fields that matter, computing memory and KV cache from them, context windows claimed and effective, licences, and a worked read of a current open-weight model. 09 Why Models Fail Hallucination as plausible continuation, calibration and sycophancy, what mechanistic interpretability has found, and what it offers practitioners today.

About this guide. A mechanism-first deep dive for engineers who use language models daily and want the machinery explained with numbers rather than metaphors. Built from the papers, model cards and tokenizer documentation cited in each chapter. Harbourline Insurance, the running example, is fictional and shared with the other AI Engineering guides.
