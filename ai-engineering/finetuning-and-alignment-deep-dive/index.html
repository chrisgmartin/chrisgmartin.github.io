Fine-Tuning and Preference Alignment

When a prompt or retrieval stops being enough, and what to do about it: choosing a base checkpoint, building post-training data you can defend, LoRA and QLoRA with the memory arithmetic, DPO and GRPO without a reinforcement-learning team, and the evals, serving and retraining that keep a tuned model honest in production.

Section A · Orient
00 Start Here Why teams fine-tune, what it can and cannot change, the vocabulary, and the two Harbourline models the guide builds.
Section B · Deciding and preparing
01 When to Fine-Tune Prompting, retrieval and fine-tuning as three different tools: what each changes in the model's behaviour, the cost model of each, the eval-first rule, and the decision for Harbourline's two candidate systems. 02 Choosing a Base Checkpoint What pre-training leaves you: base, instruct and reasoning checkpoints, the scale and data behind them, context and knowledge cutoff, licences and their terms as of November 2026, and how to shortlist and test candidates. 03 Post-Training Data Chat templates and dataset formats, mining production for examples, synthetic data from a frontier model and the terms that govern it, filtering and deduplication, how many examples you need, and the review that makes the set defensible.
Section C · Training
04 Supervised Fine-Tuning Full fine-tuning against LoRA and QLoRA with the memory arithmetic, rank, alpha and target modules, learning rate, epochs and packing, the frameworks as of November 2026, and reading a loss curve for overfitting and forgetting. 05 Preference Alignment Reward models and PPO, DPO and its variants, GRPO and reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards, rejection sampling, collecting preferences from Harbourline's adjusters, and the reward hacking to watch for. 06 Compute and Distributed Training Mixed precision, gradient checkpointing and accumulation, data parallel to FSDP and DeepSpeed ZeRO, choosing and renting GPUs, and a costed plan for Harbourline's runs.
Section D · Shipping
07 Evaluating a Tuned Model Held-out sets and paired comparison against the untuned model, capability regression on general benchmarks, safety regression after fine-tuning, judge calibration for the new behaviour, and the ship decision. 08 Serving and Retraining Merged weights against served adapters, multi-adapter serving, provider fine-tuning APIs, versioning and rollback, drift in the data the model was tuned on, and the retraining cadence. 09 Distillation and Merging Distilling a large model's behaviour into a small one, model merging methods and what they can and cannot combine, reasoning models and test-time compute, and when any of it matters for a product team.

About this guide. A mechanism-first deep dive for engineers deciding whether to fine-tune and then doing it well. Built from the papers, model cards and framework documentation cited in each chapter and from production practice.
