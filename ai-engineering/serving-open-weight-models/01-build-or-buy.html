Section B · Decide and understand

Build or Buy

API against self-hosting: the cost crossover arithmetic at Harbourline's volumes, data residency, latency, operational burden, and the hybrid most teams end up with.

The decision is a utilisation question

Per token, a rented GPU is cheap and an API is expensive, and that comparison misleads more teams than any other in this field. An API bills you for tokens you use. A GPU bills you for hours you hold, whether or not a request arrives. Self-hosting wins only when the GPUs are busy, or when something other than price forces your hand. This chapter works both questions on Harbourline's two workloads, with every figure shown, and ends on the hybrid most teams settle on.

The two workloads are fixed for the whole guide, and all Harbourline figures here are illustrative. Workload A is the nightly claims batch: about 6,000 claims a night, each about 1,500 input and 300 output tokens, so about 9 million input and 1.8 million output tokens a night, with a four-hour window and no latency target. Workload B is the help-centre assistant: about 13,000 sessions a day, treated as one request each, with an average prompt of 3,000 tokens (1,200 of them a shared system prompt) and an answer of 250 tokens. Its SLO is a time to first token of 800 ms or less at p95, at least 30 output tokens per second per stream, and UK data residency. The self-hosted candidate is Llama 3.3 70B Instruct, a dense 70B model, in FP8, two H100 80 GB GPUs to a replica.

What the API costs at these volumes

Start with the price you would pay if you did nothing. As of November 2026, Anthropic's pricing page lists Claude Sonnet 5.5 at $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, and Claude Haiku 5.5 at $0.10 and $0.50. The Batch API takes 50 percent off both, and a prompt-cache hit on Sonnet 5.5 costs 0.05 times the input price, $0.10 per million. Months are 30 days throughout.

Workload A, per night, in millions of tokens (MTok):

  • Sonnet 5.5, standard: 9 MTok × $2 + 1.8 MTok × $10 = $18 + $18 = $36. With Batch, $18, which fits a latency-insensitive job.
  • Haiku 5.5, standard: 9 × $0.10 + 1.8 × $0.50 = $0.90 + $0.90 = $1.80. With Batch, $0.90.

Workload B, per month. Input is 13,000 × 30 × 3,000 = 1,170 MTok and output is 13,000 × 30 × 250 = 97.5 MTok, 1,267.5 MTok in all.

  • Sonnet 5.5: 1,170 × $2 + 97.5 × $10 = $2,340 + $975 = $3,315, which is $2.62 per million tokens blended.
  • Sonnet 5.5 with the 1,200-token system prompt cached: 13,000 × 30 × 1,200 = 468 MTok moves from $2 to $0.10, saving 468 × $1.90 = $889, so $2,426, or $1.91 per million. Cache-write charges are ignored here; they are small when the prefix is hit every few minutes.
  • Haiku 5.5: 1,170 × $0.10 + 97.5 × $0.50 = $117 + $48.75 = $166, or $0.13 per million.

What the H100 fleet costs

The self-hosted fleet is billed by the hour. The site's neocloud pricing comparison lists on-demand H100 80 GB list prices as of November 2026, from $3.90 per GPU-hour at Crusoe (rising to $4.50 on 1 December) and $3.99 at RunPod Secure Cloud and Together to $4.50 at Nebius and $4.59 to $4.89 at Lambda. The rate used is $3.99, the figure the capacity chapter uses. Together's H100 page gives the $3.99 rate with no regional qualifier and names the UK among its European locations, while its capacity note lists only France, the Netherlands, Sweden and Romania; RunPod's published data centres include six EU sites and none in the UK. Confirm a UK cluster with the provider's sales team before relying on the rate.

One replica is two H100s holding the FP8 weights. A single replica has no failover, since a driver fault, an out-of-memory crash or a rolling upgrade takes the assistant down, so production runs two replicas, four GPUs. Four GPUs × 730 hours × $3.99 = $11,651 a month, $139,812 a year, whatever traffic arrives. One replica alone is $5,826 a month. Chapter 08 sizes the same two-replica fleet at $3.99 and arrives at the same $11,651 a month.

Now compare against Workload B. The fleet costs $11,651 a month against $2,426 to $3,315 for Sonnet 5.5 and $166 for Haiku 5.5. On price alone, Harbourline pays about $8,300 to $9,200 a month more than the Sonnet API, roughly $100,000 to $110,000 a year, and about $11,500 a month more than Haiku, before anyone is paid to run it. If anything justifies self-hosting Workload B, it is not the invoice.

Utilisation is the hidden variable

The reason is idle hardware. The capacity chapter sizes the fleet in full; here is the summary. Prefill needs about 2 × 70 × 109 = 140 GFLOP per prompt token, so a 3,000-token prompt is 420 TFLOP. The pair's dense FP8 peak is 2 × 1,979 = 3,958 TFLOPS (NVIDIA's 3,958 headline for one H100 SXM assumes structured sparsity, so dense is half). At an assumed 40 percent of peak that prefill takes 0.265 s. Decode at the p95 SLO adds 250 tokens at about 1,170 tokens a second per replica, which is 0.21 s, from the decode-step floor derated in chapter 08. A request occupies a replica for about 0.48 s, so a replica serves about 2.1 requests a second and the two replicas 4.2. Over a month that is about 35.7 billion tokens of capacity.

Harbourline sends 1,267.5 million, so utilisation is 1,267.5 ÷ 35,700 = 3.6 percent. The fleet at full load costs $11,651 ÷ 35,700 MTok = $0.33 per million tokens, and every point of utilisation below 100 percent divides that:

Against uncached Sonnet at $2.62 per million, break-even is 12.5 percent utilisation, 4.4 billion tokens a month, about 45,000 requests a day, 3.5 times today's volume. Against cached Sonnet at $1.91 it is 17 percent, about 62,000 requests a day. Against Haiku at $0.13 it never arrives, because even a saturated fleet costs $0.33. Add the people, as an illustrative quarter of one engineer at $150,000 a year loaded, $3,125 a month, and the fleet costs $14,776 and break-even against uncached Sonnet moves to 15.8 percent.

Benchmark throughput is not your utilisation

A vendor figure for tokens per second per GPU is a saturation number. Dividing the GPU-hour by it gives the cost of a fleet that never idles, which no interactive service achieves. Your cost per token is that figure divided by the share of capacity you actually use, and an interactive workload that follows office hours sits well below its monthly average at night.

Workload A shows the other side. Its 9 MTok in and 1.8 MTok out need 9,000,000 × 140 GFLOP = 1.26 × 1018 FLOP of prefill, 796 s at 1,583 TFLOPS, plus about 1,540 s of decode on one replica: roughly 39 minutes, well inside the four-hour window. Rented alone for an hour, a pair costs 2 × $3.99 = $7.98 a night. That beats Sonnet's Batch price of $18 only if the job, including model load, stays under 2.25 hours, and loses to Haiku's $0.90 at any length over seven minutes. But Workload B's fleet is idle at night and the batch can run there, so the marginal cost is the scheduling work around the daytime peak.

Residency, latency and operational burden

Three things can override the cost table, and for Harbourline's Workload B the first one does.

Data residency and contract

The assistant answers from policy documents, and Harbourline's customers include UK policyholders, so the brief requires UK residency. Anthropic's data residency page says the first-party API's inference_geo parameter accepts "global" or "us" only, and that the only workspace geo is "us", as of November 2026. A UK-only inference region is therefore not offered there. The same page says that on Amazon Bedrock and on Claude Models on Google Cloud (Google managed) the region follows the endpoint or inference profile you choose, so the partner route may offer a UK or other region, which this guide did not verify for each model and tier. Check the specific model, region and tier before you rely on it, and check what the contract says about retention and sub-processors, since a region is only one of the terms a regulator or customer can ask about. On Bedrock, London (eu-west-2) has no in-region Claude as of November 2026: Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5 are reachable through the EU geographic and global cross-region inference profiles, and Fable 5.2 through the global profile only, so inference may leave the UK. On Vertex AI, Claude in Europe is served through the EU multi-region endpoint rather than a London endpoint, and Google states that endpoints do not guarantee in-region processing.

Residency is the strongest reason to self-host, and the cheapest way to meet it is a fleet in a UK region of whichever provider you use. It also decides what the hybrid below may send where: personal data on Workload B must not spill to a non-UK API when the pair is full.

Latency

With an API, time to first token includes the network and a provider queue you do not control, and you cannot change it. With your own GPUs you control the queue and pay for the headroom that keeps it short. The 800 ms p95 TTFT budget for Workload B must absorb the 0.265 s of prefill computed above plus queueing, and queueing grows without bound as utilisation approaches 100 percent. That is another reason to keep prefill utilisation at peak under about half, not near 90 percent.

Operational burden

The API hides an on-call rotation. Self-hosting hands it to you: engine and driver upgrades, tokeniser and chat-template changes that alter outputs, GPU faults, capacity for peaks, out-of-memory incidents from long prompts, and security patching of the serving stack. The operations chapter lists them. The quarter-engineer assumption above is a floor for a team that has done this before. The other side of the ledger: you pin the weights and the engine, so nothing changes under you, whereas API models are deprecated on the vendor's schedule, as the deprecation timelines section covers. If a pinned model matters for audit, that is a real benefit.

The hybrid most teams end up with

Harbourline's answer follows from the numbers, and it is a split. The cost table says self-hosting loses on both workloads taken alone. A non-cost reason has to tip each one.

  • Workload B on the UK fleet. Residency is the tipping reason. The premium is about $8,300 to $9,200 a month over Sonnet before staff; write it down as the price of the constraint.
  • Workload A on the same fleet, overnight. No residency rule is stated for it, so the reason is idle capacity that is already paid for: about 39 minutes of work that adds almost nothing to the bill, and lifts utilisation from 3.6 percent to 4.5 percent (1,591.5 ÷ 35,700). Run alone on rented GPUs it would lose to Haiku Batch. If the claim text must also stay in the UK, residency tips it too.
  • Everything else on the API. The claims-intake agent and other surfaces that need frontier reasoning, spikes beyond the fleet's capacity, and any workload with no residency rule. Haiku 5.5 takes cheap high-volume classification, because no self-hosted 70B model undercuts its price.

The gateway that routes between them is the circuit-breaker and hedging pattern from the gateway guide: when the fleet is unhealthy or full, fall back to an API model, but only for traffic whose residency rules permit it. A UK replica held in reserve is the fallback for traffic that does not. The latency and cost interview chapter frames the same trade in answer form.

The decision flips in four ways. Volume on Workload B rises above roughly 45,000 requests a day, and the fleet beats uncached Sonnet without the residency rule. A fine-tuned adapter or a custom decoding constraint that the API cannot offer makes self-hosting the only route. A frontier-quality requirement that a 70B model fails on your labelled set moves the work back to the API, whatever the invoice says. A small API model passes your quality gate, since Haiku 5.5 is the price to beat, not Sonnet.

✓ Check yourself

Answer from memory before opening each one.

  1. Traffic to the help-centre assistant doubles to 26,000 requests a day. A colleague says the fleet is now clearly cheaper than Sonnet 5.5 through the API. Check the claim.

    Show answer

    Doubling gives 2,535 MTok a month, so the Sonnet bill is $6,630 (about $4,850 with the cached prefix) against a fixed $11,651 for the fleet. Utilisation is 7.1 percent, still well below the 12.5 percent break-even. The API is still cheaper. See utilisation.

  2. A vendor benchmark shows 3,000 output tokens a second for two H100s, and the pair costs $7.98 an hour. Why is $7.98 ÷ (3,000 × 3,600 ÷ 106) ≈ $0.74 per million output tokens the wrong number for a business case?

    Show answer

    It assumes the pair is saturated every second of every hour. Harbourline's Workload B uses 3.6 percent of the fleet's capacity, so the real cost per token is the saturated figure divided by the utilisation, about 28 times higher. A saturation figure also ignores prefill and the p95 latency budget. See utilisation.

  3. Legal asks you to guarantee that assistant prompts are processed only in the UK. What does the first-party Anthropic API offer, and what do you check before moving the assistant to a partner platform?

    Show answer

    As of November 2026 the first-party API accepts only "global" and "us" for inference, with a US-only workspace geo, so it cannot give a UK-only guarantee. On partner platforms the region follows the endpoint or inference profile, so check where the model is actually served (as of November 2026, Bedrock London reaches Claude only through EU or global cross-region profiles, and Vertex serves Europe through its EU multi-region endpoint), what the contract says about retention and sub-processors, and whether failover can cross regions. See residency.

  4. The nightly batch costs $18 on Sonnet 5.5 Batch and $0.90 on Haiku 5.5 Batch. When would you still run it on self-hosted GPUs, and when not?

    Show answer

    Run it self-hosted when the fleet is already paid for and idle overnight, so the marginal cost is near zero, or when the claim text must stay in the UK. Do not when it needs a separate rented pair at $7.98 an hour: Haiku Batch costs a tenth of that for an hour, and Sonnet Batch breaks even only under 2.25 hours. The quality gate on the labelled set decides between the API models. See utilisation.

  5. Workload B has a lunchtime spike to three times the average rate. The fleet is saturated and requests queue. A teammate proposes spilling the overflow to Sonnet 5.5. What do you ask before agreeing?

    Show answer

    Whether the spilled prompts carry personal data covered by the UK residency rule, because the spill breaks it. If they do, hold a spare UK replica or shed load; if not, route only the exempt traffic. Also ask whether answer quality differs enough to confuse users, and whether the p95 TTFT budget survives the extra hop. See the hybrid.

Takeaway

  • An API bills for tokens used and a GPU bills for hours held, so the self-hosted cost per token is the saturated cost divided by utilisation.
  • At Harbourline's Workload B the two-replica fleet runs at 3.6 percent utilisation and costs $11,651 a month, $9.19 per million tokens, against $2.62 for Sonnet 5.5 and $0.13 for Haiku 5.5.
  • Against Sonnet 5.5 the break-even is about 12.5 percent utilisation, 45,000 requests a day, and against Haiku 5.5 a 70B model on H100s never breaks even at November 2026 list prices.
  • UK residency tips Workload B to self-hosting, idle overnight capacity tips Workload A, and the premium for the first should be written down and approved.
  • The workable design is a hybrid: the UK fleet for residency-bound traffic, the nightly batch on its idle hours, and an API for frontier work and permitted overflow.
  • Every figure here is a list price or an estimate, and the capacity of the fleet has to be measured on your prompts before you commit.
