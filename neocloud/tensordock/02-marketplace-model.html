Section A · The marketplace

Marketplace Model

TensorDock's marketplace differs from Vast in how supply is curated and onboarded. The result is a smaller but more uniformly reliable supply pool.

Supply curation

TensorDock vets hosts before listing and holds them to a stated 99.99% uptime standard, with maintenance scheduled at least two weeks ahead (as of November 2026). Hosting quality comes in two tiers, and the tier is not uniform. Standards cover:

  • Top hosts sit in data centers. Standard hosts "may be hosting servers in residential or business facilities" with little or no redundancy, and some data-center hosts choose the standard label to face lower downtime penalties (Downtime Compensation docs, as of November 2026).
  • Minimum bandwidth and reliability commitments.
  • Standard hardware specifications.

This is meaningfully more restrictive than Vast's "list whatever GPU you have" approach.

Verification

TensorDock benchmarks and monitors providers continuously. The supply that ends up in the marketplace is more consistent in performance than Vast's average listing, though the standard tier gives weaker guarantees. The site says it aggregates GPUs from Tier 3/4 data centers with certifications such as SOC 2, PCI DSS, ISO 27001 and HIPAA, and marks them with a Data Center badge on the deploy page. It also says it offers converted mining rigs for price-to-performance, so check the badge before you deploy. Downtime credits are 10 times the hourly rate on top hosts and 5 times on standard hosts (docs updated June 2024).

vs Vast's open marketplace

The structural difference:

  • Vast: maximum supply density, variable quality, lowest prices.
  • TensorDock: more curated supply, more consistent quality, slightly higher prices.

The supply-curation tradeoff

The tradeoff is real. Curation produces consistency but limits supply growth. TensorDock's smaller supply pool reflects this. The bet is that customers value consistency enough to pay for it.

✓ Check yourself

Answer from memory before opening each one.

  1. Why does continuous monitoring matter more to the product than the checks a host passes at entry?

    Show answer

    An entry check measures a host once, while hardware, networks and operators drift afterwards. Continuous benchmarks catch the drift and let the platform delist or reprice the host, which keeps the average listing steady. That steadiness is what the premium buys. See verification.

  2. A customer needs 64 RTX 4090s in one region next week. Which marketplace is more likely to fill the order, and why might the customer still choose the other?

    Show answer

    Vast is more likely to fill it, because its open listing gives the denser supply. The customer might still choose TensorDock if a failed node costs more than the price gap, though the thinner pool may leave the order unfilled. See vs Vast and the tradeoff.

  3. TensorDock's growth team proposes relaxing host standards to double the supply. What happens to the business, and why?

    Show answer

    Average quality drifts toward Vast's, which removes the reason to pay the premium. TensorDock would then compete on price against a larger rival with a deeper pool. See the supply-curation tradeoff.

  4. Two listings offer the same GPU, and the Vast one is 15% cheaper. What should you compare beyond the hourly rate before choosing?

    Show answer

    Compare the expected cost of interruption: uptime history, network quality, how failures are handled and how fast support answers. A cheap hour that restarts a job is not cheap. The premium is a payment for lower expected failure cost. See vs Vast.

Takeaway

  • TensorDock vets hosts against operating standards on bandwidth, reliability and hardware before it lists them. Only top hosts must be in data centers. Standard hosts may be residential or business sites.
  • It benchmarks and monitors hosts continuously, so the typical listing performs more evenly than the average Vast listing.
  • Vast trades quality variance for supply density and the lowest price. TensorDock trades density for consistency at a slightly higher price.
  • Curation caps supply growth, so a smaller pool is the direct cost of the consistency.
  • The model pays only if enough customers value consistency above the price gap.
