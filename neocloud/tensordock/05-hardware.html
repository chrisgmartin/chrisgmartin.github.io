Section C · Hardware

Hardware Mix

TensorDock's hardware spans consumer and datacenter GPUs, with curation toward higher-quality listings. The breadth is smaller than Vast but consistency is better.

Hardware mix

Available hardware includes:

  • Consumer NVIDIA: RTX 3090 and 4090 are on the price list as of November 2026. The RTX 5090 we listed earlier is not on the pages we fetched.
  • Workstation: RTX 6000 Ada, A6000, A4000 and L40.
  • Datacenter: A100 (SXM4 and PCIe) and H100 SXM5, plus V100. The H200 we listed earlier is not on the pages we fetched; check the dashboard.
  • The site claims 45 GPU models across partners and 100+ locations in over 20 countries.

Breadth vs Vast

Vast has broader hardware breadth (more SKUs, more consumer cards). TensorDock's selection is more concentrated on the most-used SKUs.

Network

Network varies by provider but tends to be more consistent than Vast's average. Multi-node configurations are limited; single-node only for many use cases.

✓ Check yourself

Answer from memory before opening each one.

  1. Why does a short SKU list make a marketplace more consistent as well as narrower?

    Show answer

    Each SKU is a configuration the platform must benchmark, validate and support, so fewer SKUs means each gets checked properly and buyers know what to expect. Every added SKU adds testing and support load. See breadth vs Vast.

  2. You must train a 70B model across 32 GPUs. Why is TensorDock a poor first call, and what do you ask before ruling it out?

    Show answer

    Multi-node offers are limited, and distributed training needs a fast fabric between nodes. Ask whether it can provide 32 GPUs in one site on high-speed interconnect. If not, go to a cluster provider. See network.

  3. A 13B-parameter model is stored in 16-bit precision. Does it fit on a 24 GB RTX 4090 for inference, and what does that say about when you need datacenter cards?

    Show answer

    The weights alone take about 26 GB, so it does not fit. Models larger than a consumer card's memory, or served at large batch sizes, need 80 GB datacenter cards such as the A100 or H100. See hardware mix.

  4. TensorDock is adding datacenter GPUs in growing share. What does that shift do to its position against Vast, for better and for worse?

    Show answer

    It moves TensorDock toward buyers with larger budgets and tighter reliability needs, away from Vast's cheap consumer long tail. The gain is higher revenue per customer; the risk is more capital per GPU and more exposure to larger cloud rivals. See hardware mix.

Takeaway

  • The price list as of November 2026 spans consumer cards (RTX 3090, 4090), workstation cards (RTX 6000 Ada, A6000, A4000, L40) and datacenter cards (V100, A100, H100). The site claims 45 models in all.
  • Vast lists more SKUs and more consumer cards. TensorDock concentrates on the most-used ones.
  • A short SKU list is easier to validate and compare, so it supports consistency, but it narrows choice.
  • Network quality varies by provider and is steadier than Vast's average.
  • Multi-node configurations are limited, and many offers are single-node only, which rules out large distributed training.
