Section B · Operations

Product Surface

TensorDock offers pods, dedicated servers, and standard cloud-style management. The product is functional and comparable to other marketplace competitors.

Pods and instances

The primary product is on-demand GPU instances launchable through the web UI or API. Pre-built templates support common workloads.

Dedicated servers

For longer-term commitments, dedicated server rental is available. Providers reserve specific hardware for a customer for the contract duration.

On-demand vs reserved

Like other marketplaces, on-demand and reserved tiers exist. Reserved pricing offers meaningful discounts.

Developer experience

The DX is functional but not as polished as RunPod's. Web UI is clean; CLI/API for automation; documentation is adequate.

✓ Check yourself

Answer from memory before opening each one.

  1. A team runs a ten-hour fine-tune once a week and keeps one inference endpoint up all month. Which tier fits each job, and why?

    Show answer

    Use on-demand for the fine-tune, because you pay only for the ten hours. Use reserved or dedicated for the endpoint, because near-100% utilisation turns the reserved discount into a straight saving. See on-demand vs reserved.

  2. What does a dedicated server give a customer that an on-demand instance does not, and what does the customer give up for it?

    Show answer

    It reserves specific hardware for the contract term, so capacity cannot disappear and performance stays predictable. The customer commits to the term and pays whether or not the machine is busy. See dedicated servers.

  3. Reserved pricing here is 25% below on-demand per hour but bills for every hour of the month. Above what utilisation does reserved win?

    Show answer

    It wins above 75% utilisation, since 0.75 of the on-demand hourly rate times all hours equals on-demand cost at 75% of the hours. Below that, on-demand is cheaper. Real discounts differ, but the method holds. See on-demand vs reserved.

  4. TensorDock's developer experience trails RunPod's. For which customers does that gap matter most, and for which hardly at all?

    Show answer

    It matters most to small teams and newcomers who want templates and a fast start, because they have no one to smooth the rough edges. It matters little to customers who automate through the API and choose on price and reliability. See developer experience.

Takeaway

  • The main product is an on-demand GPU instance, launched from the web UI or the API, with templates for common workloads.
  • Dedicated servers cover longer commitments: specific hardware is reserved for one customer for the contract term.
  • On-demand and reserved tiers both exist, and the reserved discount is meaningful.
  • Developer experience is functional rather than polished: a clean UI, an API for automation and adequate docs, behind RunPod.
  • The product surface matches its rivals. It is not where TensorDock wins; supply curation is.
