The Company

Vast.AI was founded in 2016 by Jake Cannell and co-founders on a thesis the industry has since validated by force: GPU compute would become the scarcest commodity in tech, and a marketplace was the cheapest way to price-discover supply against demand.

Founding

Vast.AI (technically Vast.ai, all lowercase, but rendered Vast.AI for clarity in writing) was founded in 2016. The press kit gives the date as June 28, 2016. The boilerplate of one 2025 press release says 2018, and this guide follows the press kit. The founding context is essential: 2016 was years before ChatGPT, before the LLM boom, before "AI compute" was a venture category. The original users were academic ML researchers, computer-vision practitioners, and crypto miners who needed GPUs for either training or proof-of-work hashing.

The founding team came from technical backgrounds. Jake Cannell, the CEO and co-founder, is an ML engineer and GPU programmer who wrote about AI scaling before founding the company. The team saw an inefficiency: GPUs in academic labs sat idle most of the time; GPUs at hyperscalers were rented at 10x what they cost.

The company has been notably quiet about itself over the years — minimal PR, founder rarely on conference circuits, no big funding announcements. That posture is unusual in the AI-infrastructure space, where most companies cultivate visibility.

The original thesis

Three claims, all of which have proved out:

  1. GPU compute will get more important, not less. Deep learning was just starting in 2016; the team bet it would scale. They were right by orders of magnitude.
  2. Hyperscaler GPU pricing reflects margin, not cost. AWS, Azure, and GCP charge GPU rates well above the marginal cost of operating the hardware. That gap is room for a marketplace.
  3. Supply already exists — it's just not aggregated. Universities, small datacenter operators, mining farms (back when crypto-mining GPUs were a thing), and individual enthusiasts had GPU hardware sitting idle. A marketplace could turn that into rentable capacity without anyone building new datacenters.

The third point is what makes Vast structurally different from a traditional cloud. Vast doesn't operate GPUs. Vast operates a marketplace. The GPUs belong to providers — third parties who list their hardware on Vast in exchange for a share of the rental revenue.

This is a profound architectural difference from CoreWeave, Crusoe, Lambda, or any traditional cloud. Those companies own the hardware. Vast does not. The implication is that Vast can scale supply without capital expenditure — but Vast also has limited control over the quality of the supply on its platform.

Scale today

Vast publishes a few headline figures in its press kit, as of November 2026. Beyond those, the public signals point to a marketplace at meaningful scale:

  • 17,000+ GPUs on the platform, from 350+ independent hosts and 40+ data centers, according to the press kit as of November 2026. The live number fluctuates as providers join and leave; the marketplace search exposes current availability.
  • A large customer base. Vast has not published an account count. Earlier estimates of hundreds of thousands of accounts over the platform's history are unconfirmed.
  • Coverage from consumer cards (RTX 3090, 4090) up through datacenter-grade (A100, H100, H200, B200). The mix tilts more consumer than enterprise neoclouds, but the high end is well-represented.
  • Revenue at meaningful but undisclosed scale. Public estimates have put Vast at meaningful eight-figure ARR; the company hasn't confirmed.

By absolute GPU count or revenue, Vast is much smaller than CoreWeave or Crusoe. By number of unique users and breadth of available hardware models, Vast is one of the largest GPU markets in the world. The two metrics measure different things; both are real.

Ownership & funding

Vast.AI has been notably under-the-radar on funding. The press kit lists no funding, and in 2025 public records showed only seed-stage funding. The company has not announced the kind of large growth rounds that CoreWeave, Crusoe, Lambda, or Together.AI have done.

The reasonable inference is that Vast is profitable or close to it on a marketplace-take-rate business model, which doesn't require the kind of capital those competitors raise to buy GPUs. The marketplace is asset-light by design.

This shapes the strategic posture: Vast doesn't need to grow revenue at venture pace; it doesn't have a board pushing for an IPO timeline; it can be slow and deliberate about strategic moves. That's an unusual position in the 2026 neocloud landscape where most competitors are either public (CoreWeave, Nebius) or pushing hard toward exits.

Organizational shape

Small. The press kit reports 40+ employees as of November 2026, with a flat structure and a strong technical bias. Jake Cannell is CEO and Travis Cannell, who joined in 2022, is COO. The company is based in Los Angeles and opened a second office in San Francisco in June 2025. It has never grown into a sprawling org.

The leanness is intentional. A marketplace at Vast's scale can be operated by a small team — the customers (both supply and demand sides) self-serve through the web UI and API. Vast's job is to keep the marketplace running, improve trust and ranking systems, and resolve disputes. It's not a sales-led business.

Compare with CoreWeave at 1,000+ employees or Lambda at several hundred. Vast does similar gross-merchandise-volume work with a fraction of the headcount because the marketplace model needs far fewer sales, customer success and datacenter operations staff. The company now markets an enterprise offering with dedicated support, which chapter 09 covers.

Culture & posture

From the outside, Vast presents as engineering-led, anti-marketing, comfortable with niche positioning. Behaviors that signal this:

  • The website is functional rather than designed for marketing impact.
  • The product surface (CLI, API, web UI) is technical-first; no consumer onboarding flow.
  • Documentation is dense and assumes Linux + SSH + Docker familiarity.
  • Public communication is sparse. The press release page lists four announcements, the latest from June 2025.
  • The community on Discord and Reddit is active and helpful, but it's not company-managed evangelism; it's organic.

This culture has advantages and disadvantages. It keeps Vast cheap to operate, attracts a self-selecting technical user base, and avoids the trap of over-promising. It also limits Vast's ability to capture enterprise customers, who expect polish, account managers, and SLAs.

✓ Check yourself

Answer from memory before opening each one.

  1. Vast and CoreWeave both rent out H100s. Name what Vast does not own, and one consequence of that for capital needs and one for quality control.

    Show answer

    Vast does not own the GPUs; independent providers do. Supply can grow without Vast buying hardware, so capital needs stay low. The cost is control: Vast cannot guarantee what sits behind a listing and has to police quality through ranking and verification. See the original thesis.

  2. An analyst ranks Vast far below CoreWeave. Another ranks it among the largest GPU markets in the world. Can both be right?

    Show answer

    Yes, because they measure different things. Counted by GPUs owned or by revenue, Vast is much smaller. Counted by unique users and hardware variety, it is among the largest. A claim about size needs its metric attached. See scale today.

  3. A small team runs a marketplace that moves as much compute as companies with far larger headcounts. Which functions does it skip, and what does that tell you about the customers?

    Show answer

    It skips sales, customer success and datacenter operations, because both sides self-serve through the web UI, CLI and API. That works only for customers who need no account manager, contract or SLA. The staffing is a symptom of the segment. See organizational shape.

  4. Of the three claims behind the 2016 thesis, which one would erode Vast's price advantage if it stopped being true, and how?

    Show answer

    The claim that hyperscaler GPU prices reflect margin rather than cost. The gap between price and cost is the room a marketplace undercuts. If hyperscaler prices fell toward cost, the discount would shrink even with supply unchanged. See the original thesis.

  5. A board member says Vast should hire enterprise sales and a marketing team. What do you weigh before agreeing?

    Show answer

    Upside: enterprise buyers expect polish, account managers and SLAs, so a sales motion opens a segment Vast cannot reach today. Downside: it raises the cost base that makes Vast cheap, and it may alienate the technical, self-selecting base. The answer depends on whether the marketplace can serve enterprise supply at all. See culture and posture.

Takeaway

  • Vast is a marketplace: providers own and price the GPUs, and Vast matches, meters and bills. That one choice shapes every other chapter.
  • The 2016 thesis rested on three bets: GPU demand would grow, hyperscaler prices carry margin above cost, and idle supply already existed. All three have held so far.
  • Vast is small next to CoreWeave by GPUs owned and revenue, and among the largest GPU markets by users and hardware variety.
  • Asset-light economics mean little outside funding, no pressure for venture-pace growth, and a small, engineering-led team.
  • The open strategic question is whether a marketplace can reach enterprise buyers or stays capped at indie, research and experimental work.
