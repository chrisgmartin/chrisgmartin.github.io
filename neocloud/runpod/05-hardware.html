Section B · Operations

Hardware & Infrastructure

RunPod's GPU inventory spans consumer and datacenter cards across both products. The Secure Cloud datacenter footprint is small relative to enterprise neoclouds but growing.

GPU mix

Across both products, RunPod lists or operates:

  • Consumer NVIDIA: RTX 3090, 4090, 5090 (the GPU types page, November 2026).
  • Workstation: RTX A6000, RTX 6000 Ada, and RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell in several forms, including MIG slices.
  • Datacenter: A100 80GB, L40 and L40S, H100 (SXM, PCIe and NVL), H200, B200 and B300.
  • AMD: MI300X (192 GB) is listed among the GPU types with no experimental label. The pricing page does not list it.

The mix is similar in shape to Vast but the absolute count is smaller. Where Vast might have hundreds of H100 listings at any moment, RunPod might have dozens-to-low-hundreds.

Community Cloud hardware

Community Cloud hardware is whatever providers list. The mix tilts more toward small-fleet operators than Vast's more hobbyist-heavy supply:

  • More multi-GPU machines (4x and 8x configurations more common).
  • More datacenter-grade cards (A100, H100) and fewer pure-consumer 3090s.
  • More consistent bandwidth (providers are often colocated, not residential).
  • Slightly more reliable on average than Vast's average listing.

This shape is consequence of RunPod's slightly higher curation bar — the marketplace deliberately discourages purely-residential listings.

Secure Cloud hardware

Secure Cloud is RunPod-procured GPUs in RunPod-controlled or RunPod-partnered datacenters. The hardware mix is more concentrated on datacenter-grade cards:

  • Heavy on A100, H100, H200.
  • Some L40S for inference workloads.
  • B200 and B300 are on the pricing page, at $7.99 and $8.99 per hour on Secure Cloud as of 4 November 2026.
  • Multi-GPU configurations (typically 8x H100 per node) for cluster workloads.

The configurations are more uniform than Community Cloud — same hardware spec across all listings, predictable performance.

Datacenter footprint

Runpod reports 32 regions and over 30 GPU configurations (September 2026 release). The global networking page lists 17 data centers across the US, Canada, Europe, Iceland and Australia. Secure Cloud runs in T3 and T4 data centers. Runpod does not publish its power, so the old comparison with the multi-hundred-MW sites at Crusoe has no public figure on the RunPod side.

The strategy is to grow footprint where customer demand justifies it rather than building speculative capacity. This is more conservative than CoreWeave's strategy of pre-building for committed customers.

Network

Secure Cloud networking includes:

  • Global networking between Pods in one account at 100 Mbps, which is enough for light traffic and not for training.
  • Instant Clusters for multi-node training, with 1600 to 3200 Gbps between nodes (3200 on B200, H200 and H100; 1600 on A100).
  • Standard cloud egress to the public internet.

Multi-node distributed training is possible on Secure Cloud but the cluster sizes are smaller than what CoreWeave or Crusoe offer. Self-serve Instant Clusters span 2 to 8 nodes, which is 16 to 64 GPUs. Runpod says clusters of up to 512 GPUs are possible through sales. Thousands probably don't work.

Scaling supply

RunPod can scale supply two ways:

  • Recruit more Community Cloud providers. Asset-light; happens organically; bounded by how aggressively RunPod competes with Vast on supply-side terms.
  • Buy more GPUs for Secure Cloud. Capital-intensive; requires fundraising or revenue-based cap-ex investment; takes 6-12 months to deploy new capacity in a new region.

The blended approach lets RunPod grow capacity faster than a pure cloud (because Community Cloud scales without cap-ex) but more slowly than a pure marketplace (because Secure Cloud's growth is capital-constrained). Net: middling growth rate by neocloud standards.

    RunPod's higher curation bar discourages purely residential listings, so more supply comes from small colocated fleets with 4x and 8x machines. See Community Cloud hardware.

    Predictable performance. The same spec, network and operations standard hold across instances in a region, so a benchmark on one transfers to the rest. See Secure Cloud hardware.

  3. A team wants to train on 2,000 GPUs. Can RunPod host it? Explain.

    Show answer

    Probably not. Multi-node training works on Secure Cloud, but self-serve clusters run to 64 GPUs, and up to 512 through sales, not thousands. Larger runs go to CoreWeave or Crusoe. See network.

  4. RunPod can grow supply two ways. Which is faster and which is slower, and why?

    Show answer

    Recruiting Community providers is asset-light and fast. Buying GPUs for Secure Cloud needs capital and 6 to 12 months to open a region. The blend grows faster than a pure cloud and slower than a pure marketplace. See scaling supply.

  5. Why does RunPod add Secure Cloud capacity where demand exists instead of pre-building like CoreWeave?

    Show answer

    It has far less capital than CoreWeave ($100M raised at a $1.0B valuation in June 2026 against CoreWeave's tens of billions of debt) and does not pre-build speculatively. CoreWeave pre-builds against signed multi-year contracts. See datacenter footprint.

Takeaway

  • The GPU mix spans consumer, workstation and datacenter cards, with fewer units than Vast.
  • Community Cloud hardware is more multi-GPU and more consistent than Vast's average listing.
  • Secure Cloud is concentrated on A100, H100 and H200, with B200 and B300 now listed.
  • Instant Clusters support 16 to 64 GPUs self-serve and up to 512 through sales, not thousands.
  • Supply grows via asset-light recruitment or capital-heavy purchases, so growth is middling.
