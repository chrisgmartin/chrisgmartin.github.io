Pricing & Commercial

RunPod prices each product differently. Community Cloud follows marketplace dynamics; Secure Cloud follows operator economics; Serverless follows per-second worker pricing. Per-second billing across all three.

Community Cloud pricing

Community Cloud draws on a vetted peer-to-peer supply of individual compute providers. The pricing page now shows one Community Cloud rate per GPU.

Secure Cloud pricing

Secure Cloud is RunPod-priced, not marketplace-determined. The November 2026 page shows Secure Cloud about 30 to 50 percent above Community Cloud for datacenter cards such as H100 and H200, and far more for consumer cards (RTX 4090: $0.89 against $0.34), reflecting the dedicated reliability tier.

Serverless pricing

Serverless bills per second of worker time, from when a worker starts until it stops. That covers cold start (loading the model), execution, and idle wait after the last job, which defaults to 5 seconds before the worker scales down. It does not bill per request. A flex worker scales to zero, so a scaled-down worker costs nothing. Active workers run all the time and discounts go through sales.

The pricing page lists hourly rates per GPU as of 4 November 2026, for example $1.34 for a 24 GB card such as the RTX 4090, $2.72 for an A100, $5.39 for an H100, $6.99 for an H200, $10.79 for a B200 and $11.99 for a B300.

The pricing makes serverless attractive for low-utilization inference workloads. If you receive 1000 requests per day and each takes 2 seconds of GPU time, you pay for about 2000 GPU-seconds of execution. If every request also lands on a cold worker, add its cold start and the 5-second idle wait, which is at most about 7000 seconds plus the cold starts. Either way that is far below the 86400 seconds of a dedicated GPU, so sparse workloads can save ten times or more.

For high-utilization workloads, dedicated pod or Secure Cloud is cheaper on a per-hour basis.

Per-second billing

Pods and serverless workers bill by the second, rounded up to the nearest second, with no fee for data in or out. Network volumes bill hourly. This is a meaningful improvement over the hourly billing many smaller clouds use.

Impact:

  • Short jobs (a few minutes of inference, brief experimentation) don't pay for a full hour.
  • Restarted instances after preemption don't lose partial hours.
  • Per-second billing matches per-second cost; the user's bill tracks actual usage.

For workloads that spin up and tear down frequently, this can be material.

Reservations & commitments

Savings plans are deprecated and can no longer be purchased, according to the Runpod docs. A plan bought earlier stays active until it expires. The monthly, quarterly and annual discount tiers that this chapter listed in 2025 no longer appear in the docs or on the pricing page.

Committed capacity now goes through sales. The pricing page lists reserved clusters for 1, 3, 6, 12 and 12-plus month terms as "Contact sales," with discounted rates for enterprises scaling to 10,000-plus GPUs. On-demand Instant Clusters are listed at $6.35 per hour for H200 SXM and $2.15 for A100 SXM. This is the structure that competes with CoreWeave, Crusoe and Lambda, but you cannot read the discount from a public page.

Contracts & enterprise terms

For larger customers, RunPod offers enterprise contracts with:

  • Master service agreements (MSAs) with negotiated terms.
  • Custom pricing on volume.
  • Account management, named technical account managers and contractual SLAs, as announced in September 2026.
  • SOC 2 Type II and ISO 27001 as the contracting baseline, with HIPAA support under business associate agreements.
  • Optional dedicated capacity reservations.

The enterprise motion is less mature than at CoreWeave or Crusoe — RunPod's headcount and sales infrastructure are smaller. But the offering exists and lands deals at meaningful annual contract values.

Comparative position

RunPod's pricing position by competitor:

  • vs Vast (marketplace): estimated 10-25% more expensive on Community Cloud in 2025; better DX and somewhat better reliability.
  • vs Lambda (mid-tier enterprise): Comparable on Secure Cloud; RunPod has the marketplace channel Lambda doesn't.
  • vs CoreWeave (Platinum-tier enterprise): Cheaper on Secure Cloud for smaller commitments; CoreWeave wins on huge multi-year reservations with bespoke pricing.
  • vs hyperscalers: 50-60% cheaper across the board; comparable to other mid-tier neoclouds.
  • vs Together.AI / Fireworks (inference): Serverless competes head-on; pricing varies by model and workload.

Answer from memory before opening each one.

  1. A job needs 40 seconds of GPU every few minutes all day. Why is hourly billing a poor fit and what does RunPod do?

    Show answer

    Hourly billing charges a full hour for each short use. RunPod bills by the second, so the bill tracks actual use. See per-second billing.

  2. An endpoint gets 1,000 requests a day at 2 seconds of GPU each. Roughly how many GPU seconds do you pay on Serverless versus a dedicated pod, and when does the answer flip?

    Show answer

    About 2,000 seconds of execution plus cold starts and idle wait, at most about 7,000 and a few cold starts, against 86,400. It flips as utilisation rises, because a dedicated pod costs less per hour than worker time: H100 SXM is $3.99 on Secure Cloud and $5.39 on Serverless. See Serverless pricing.

  3. Secure Cloud costs about half the hyperscaler list price. What does the buyer give up?

    Show answer

    Ecosystem breadth such as S3, IAM and hundreds of services, plus enterprise procurement and compliance breadth. The discount fits AI-first workloads, not ones embedded in a wider cloud. See Secure Cloud pricing.

  4. Why is a Community Cloud price quoted as a range, and what does that mean when you budget?

    Show answer

    Community Cloud draws on independent providers, so the rate can change with supply and demand. The page shows one rate per GPU today; recheck it at launch. See Community Cloud pricing.

  5. Your team will run a steady Secure Cloud workload for a year. What commercial path does the chapter describe, and what do you verify first?

    Show answer

    Savings plans are deprecated, so the path is a reserved cluster quoted by sales, with an MSA and a SOC 2 Type II baseline. Ask for the discount in writing, because no public page states it. See reservations.

Takeaway

  • Community Cloud follows marketplace pricing, Secure Cloud follows operator pricing and Serverless follows per-second compute pricing.
  • Community Cloud was estimated at 10 to 25 percent above Vast in 2025; Secure Cloud is about half the 2025 hyperscaler list.
  • Per-second billing across pods and serverless workers helps short and bursty jobs. Serverless bills cold start and idle wait, not only execution.
  • Serverless saves most on sparse traffic and loses to a dedicated pod at high utilisation.
  • Savings plans are deprecated; reserved clusters and enterprise terms go through sales and are less mature than at CoreWeave or Crusoe.
