Section A · Origin & scale

The Company

CoreWeave originated as Atlantic Crypto, a 2017 Ethereum-mining operation. Pivoted to AI compute in 2019-2020. IPO in 2025. The most-watched neocloud in the industry, with multi-billion-dollar customer commitments and an unusually deep NVIDIA relationship.

Origin: from Atlantic Crypto

The company was founded in 2017 as Atlantic Crypto, a cryptocurrency mining operation focused on Ethereum at the height of the mid-decade crypto cycle. The founders (Michael Intrator, Brian Venturo, Brannin McBee) ran GPU-based Ethereum mining at scale.

This origin matters for understanding CoreWeave today. The team built deep operational expertise in:

  • Procuring GPUs at scale.
  • Building datacenter and power infrastructure to run GPUs efficiently.
  • Optimizing software stacks for GPU utilization.
  • Managing thousands of GPU machines as a unified fleet.

These are exactly the operational competencies a GPU cloud needs. When the AI pivot happened, CoreWeave had a 2-3 year head start on the operational know-how that pure-AI-startup competitors had to learn from scratch.

The AI pivot

By 2019-2020, the founders recognized that AI compute demand was about to grow faster than crypto-mining economics could justify. The pivot involved:

  • Renaming from Atlantic Crypto to CoreWeave.
  • Repositioning the company as a GPU cloud for AI / ML workloads.
  • Beginning to acquire datacenter-grade NVIDIA cards (V100, then A100).
  • Building the software stack to support cloud-style GPU rental.
  • Cultivating direct relationships with NVIDIA for allocation.

The pivot timing turned out to be excellent. By the time ChatGPT launched in November 2022 and AI compute demand exploded, CoreWeave was operationally ready in a way the broader cloud industry wasn't.

Founders

The founding team has stayed with the company through the AI transition and the IPO:

  • Michael Intrator — CEO. Background in commodities trading. Drives the deal-making and capital strategy.
  • Brian Venturo — Chief Strategy Officer. Background in technology operations. Drives the platform engineering and product.
  • Brannin McBee — Chief Development Officer. Background in commercial real estate / energy.

The trio's mix of commercial, technical, and infrastructure backgrounds maps directly onto what running CoreWeave requires. The complementarity has been a strategic asset.

Funding through 2024

CoreWeave's funding history through the pre-IPO years involved multiple rounds at progressively higher valuations:

  • Early seed and Series A funding in 2019-2021.
  • Significant Series B (~$200M) led by Magnetar Capital in 2022.
  • Series C funding in 2023, with NVIDIA participating directly — a meaningful signal of strategic alignment.
  • Late-stage rounds in 2024 at very high valuations as the AI demand surge continued.
  • Multi-billion dollar debt facilities backed by GPU collateral. This is unique in the industry — using GPU inventory as collateral for the debt that funds GPU purchases.

Total capital raised pre-IPO was in the multi-billion-dollar range across equity and debt — the largest funding stack of any neocloud.

The 2025 IPO

CoreWeave went public on Nasdaq in 2025 under ticker CRWV. The IPO was one of the most-watched tech offerings of the year. Initial valuation was substantial; the stock has traded with volatility reflecting the strategic uncertainty around the neocloud category.

Public-company status changes CoreWeave's posture in several ways:

  • Quarterly financial disclosures. The company's revenue, customer concentration, and capital structure are now public.
  • Investor pressure. Quarterly earnings cycles create incentives that private-company peers don't face.
  • Currency for M&A. Public stock can be used to acquire smaller competitors or strategic assets.
  • Visibility. CoreWeave is the most-discussed neocloud in financial media; the narrative around the entire category often centers on CoreWeave's results.

Scale today

By essentially every public metric, CoreWeave is the largest pure-play neocloud. The figures below come from the 2025 10-K and the June 2026 10-Q, as of November 2026:

  • Revenue of $5.1 billion in 2025, up from $1.9 billion in 2024 and $229 million in 2023.
  • A large GPU fleet across 43 data centers. The 10-K reports power, not a GPU count.
  • Over 850 MW of active power at 31 December 2025, up from about 360 MW a year earlier, with about 3.1 GW contracted. Sites are in the US, UK, Spain, Sweden and Norway.
  • $103.7 billion of remaining performance obligations (contracted revenue not yet recognised) at 30 June 2026, up from $60.7 billion at 31 December 2025. Microsoft, Meta and OpenAI are covered in later chapters.
  • Platinum tier in SemiAnalysis's ClusterMAX 3.0 rating (September 2026). Nebius joined it in 3.0, so CoreWeave no longer stands alone there.
  • 2,189 employees at 31 December 2025.

The company grew revenue at triple-digit-percent annual rates through 2023-2025. Revenue for the first half of 2026 was $4.65 billion, up about 112% on the first half of 2025. That growth rate will compress as the base gets larger, but the absolute scale is now meaningful in the broader cloud market.

✓ Check yourself

Answer from memory before opening each one.

  1. A colleague calls CoreWeave's Ethereum-mining past a footnote. Which skills carried over to the GPU cloud, and what advantage did they give over AI-native startups?

    Show answer

    Buying GPUs at scale, building power and datacenter capacity, tuning software for utilisation, and running thousands of machines as one fleet. That gave CoreWeave a head start of roughly two to three years on operations that AI-native entrants had to learn from nothing. See the origin.

  2. ChatGPT launched in November 2022. What had CoreWeave already done by then that a company starting that month could not have done?

    Show answer

    It had renamed itself, moved from mining to datacenter cards (V100, then A100), built a software stack for renting GPUs by the hour, and cultivated NVIDIA for allocation. Buying capacity and learning to operate it takes years, so the demand surge landed on a prepared company. See the AI pivot.

  3. CoreWeave borrows against GPUs to buy more GPUs. What makes this structure unusual, and what condition has to hold for it to work?

    Show answer

    The collateral is the same asset class the debt purchases, which no other neocloud did at this scale. It works while the GPUs keep earning enough, through contracted revenue, to service the debt and keep their value. See funding.

  4. You are comparing a public CoreWeave with a private neocloud as a supplier. What does the listing change for you as a customer, and what does it change for management?

    Show answer

    As a customer you can read revenue, customer concentration and capital structure in filings, which a private peer does not publish. Management gains stock as acquisition currency and faces quarterly earnings pressure. See the IPO.

  5. An analyst extrapolates 2023 to 2025 growth rates through 2030. Why is that wrong, and what does the absolute number still tell you?

    Show answer

    Growth rates compress as the base grows, because the same percentage needs far more new revenue. The absolute scale is still large enough to matter in the wider cloud market. See scale today.

Takeaway

  • CoreWeave began as Atlantic Crypto, an Ethereum-mining firm, and the mining years built the skills a GPU cloud needs: procurement, power, utilisation and fleet operations.
  • The pivot to AI compute in 2019 and 2020 left it ready when demand surged after ChatGPT in late 2022.
  • Its funding stack leans on debt secured by GPUs, which speeds growth and ties the company's health to GPU earnings and values.
  • The 2025 IPO makes revenue, concentration and debt public, and adds quarterly pressure.
  • Scale is the largest of any pure-play neocloud, but growth rates will compress as the base grows.
