Section C · Industry frames

Hyperscaler vs Neocloud Dynamics

The coopetition dynamic — hyperscalers as both customers and competitors of neoclouds — is the defining feature of the industry's competitive structure.

Coopetition

The standard tech-industry coopetition dynamic at unusual intensity:

  • Hyperscalers (Microsoft, AWS, Google) operate their own GPU compute and compete with neoclouds for enterprise customers.
  • Same hyperscalers buy capacity from neoclouds at very large scale.
  • Some customers (OpenAI, etc.) buy from both simultaneously.
  • The competitive lines shift over time based on capacity, demand, and strategic posture.

Why hyperscalers buy

Several reasons hyperscalers source from neoclouds even while building internally:

  1. Speed. Building new datacenter capacity takes 18-24 months. Buying from a neocloud who already has it is faster.
  2. Capital structure. Neoclouds absorb GPU depreciation risk on the neocloud's balance sheet.
  3. Allocation access. Neocloud's NVIDIA relationships sometimes deliver capacity faster than hyperscaler-direct procurement.
  4. Customer pass-through. Customers (OpenAI for Microsoft, similar) drive demand that the hyperscaler may need to fulfill faster than internal builds allow.
  5. Strategic optionality. Reduces dependence on the hyperscaler's own datacenter build pace.

Why they also build

Hyperscalers continue heavy internal capacity investment because:

  1. Long-term cost advantage of owned capacity.
  2. Strategic independence from third-party suppliers.
  3. Custom silicon advantages (Microsoft's Maia, Google's TPU, AWS's Trainium). Microsoft introduced Maia 200, an inference accelerator, on 26 January 2026. By October 2026 it said Maia 200 was in production in the United States, Europe and Australia, and that Maia 300 production begins in 2027.
  4. Integration with the broader cloud stack.
  5. Scale that no single neocloud can match.

The shifting balance

The buy-vs-build balance is dynamic. Through 2025-2026, hyperscalers have been buying heavily because internal capacity hadn't caught up to AI demand. As internal capacity ramps, the buy share may decline.

This dynamic is the biggest single risk to neoclouds that depend on hyperscaler commitments. CoreWeave's customer concentration is exposed to this: Microsoft was about 67% of its 2025 revenue, and its largest customer was 36% of revenue in the second quarter of 2026, when the 10-Q names no customer.

✓ Check yourself

Answer from memory before opening each one.

  1. Microsoft is building datacenters and also buying from CoreWeave. Give two reasons the purchase makes sense.

    Show answer

    Speed, since a build takes 18 to 24 months and a neocloud already has capacity. Capital structure, since the neocloud carries GPU depreciation risk on its balance sheet. See why hyperscalers buy.

  2. What would make a hyperscaler cut its neocloud purchases?

    Show answer

    Internal capacity catching up to AI demand. As owned capacity ramps, the long-term cost advantage and independence of building it favor reducing external buys. See the shifting balance.

  3. How does the buy-or-build balance create risk for CoreWeave specifically?

    Show answer

    A large share of its revenue and backlog is hyperscaler commitments, so a drop in external buying hits it more than a diversified provider. Customer concentration turns a hyperscaler's build plan into CoreWeave's revenue risk. See the shifting balance.

  4. Custom silicon like Maia, TPU and Trainium is listed as a reason to build. What does that imply for neocloud GPU fleets?

    Show answer

    Hyperscaler workloads may move to chips the neocloud does not carry, reducing demand for NVIDIA capacity from those buyers. Neoclouds built on NVIDIA lose the part of demand that moves. See why they also build.

  5. OpenAI buys from both Microsoft and neoclouds. What does that do to the competitive lines?

    Show answer

    It blurs them. A customer can demand capacity from the neocloud through the hyperscaler or direct, so the same capacity can be a partnership in one deal and a rivalry in the next. See coopetition.

Takeaway

  • Hyperscalers are both competitors and customers of neoclouds, at very large scale.
  • They buy for speed, capital structure, allocation access, customer pass-through and optionality.
  • They also build for cost, independence, custom silicon, stack integration and scale.
  • Through 2025 and 2026 buying dominates because internal capacity lags AI demand.
  • As internal capacity ramps the buy share may fall, which is the biggest single risk to neoclouds that depend on hyperscaler commitments.
