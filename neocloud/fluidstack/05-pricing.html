Section C · How it pays

Pricing & Commercial

Contract structures, what list prices exist, and how the economics compare with rivals.

Two prices, one company

Fluidstack has had two pricing models and they do not overlap in time. Until 2025 it sold GPU hours and published a rate card like any neocloud. Since 2025 it sells capacity under long contracts whose prices are private, and in November 2026 the public rate card is gone. A reader who quotes an H100 price for Fluidstack is quoting history; a reader who asks what Fluidstack charges Anthropic will get no answer from any public source. This chapter sets out what can be known about each.

The list prices that existed

Aggregators that scraped the old pricing page carry H100 SXM at $2.10 and H200 SXM at $2.30 per GPU-hour, with GB200 NVL72 "on request"; one lists an 8-way A100 80 GB server at $1.30 per GPU-hour. The term and date of those figures were never captured, two other aggregators show different H100 numbers, and a comparison site noted in September 2026 that Fluidstack lists no prices. The page itself returns a 404.

Those numbers are still useful as a marker. At $2.10 an H100 was well under the $3.90 to $4.89 on-demand list prices that the comparison chapter records for Crusoe, RunPod, Together, Nebius and Lambda in November 2026, and close to SemiAnalysis' one-year H100 rental index, which it reported at $1.70 in October 2025 and $2.35 in March 2026. A low list price for a company with little on-demand capacity is consistent with the rate card being a door to a sales conversation rather than a product, and with the pivot described in chapter 02.

How contract capacity is priced

No Fluidstack customer price is public. What is public is the cost side, from the landlord filings, and the shape of the customer contracts, from the announcements. Together they bound the price.

  • Rent per megawatt. River Bend is $7.0 billion over 15 years for 245 MW of IT load, about $1.9 million per MW per year before the 3.0% escalator. Lake Mariner is about $6.7 billion over 10 years for over 360 MW, about $1.85 million per MW-year. Barber Lake's original $3 billion over 10 years for 168 MW is about $1.8 million. The three landlords priced within about 5% of one another, which suggests a market rate for a powered, built AI hall of roughly $1.8 to $1.9 million per MW-year on a triple-net basis as of 2025.
  • What sits on top. Fluidstack must recover the fit-out it funds, its operating staff, the backstop's cost to Google if any is passed through, and a margin. The customer either brings chips (TPUs, in Anthropic's case) or pays for them inside the contract.
  • Term. Customer commitments must be at least as long as the lease base terms they sit on, ten to fifteen years, or Fluidstack would carry the gap; the $50 billion Anthropic programme and the Hut 8 partnership "up to 2.295 GW" are the size of commitment that implies.

Forbes' line that $50 billion is "roughly the cost of about 1 GW" gives the all-in figure with chips. Against rent of under $2 million per MW-year, the building is a small share; the chips and the power are the cost, and the operator's margin is what it earns for delivering them on time.

The unit economics

The investor memo reported by Forbes projected revenue of about $200 million in 2025 and $660 million in 2026, against a plan to manage up to 1.3 GW in 2026. If both are right, 2026 revenue is around $0.5 million per contracted megawatt, far below the rent per megawatt in the leases, which can only mean that most contracted capacity is not yet energised and that revenue recognised lags the lease commitments by a year or more. That lag is the financial shape of a builder: commitments now, revenue when the halls open, and rent payable from lease commencement whether or not the customer's chips have arrived. Chapter 09 returns to it.

The older business had different economics. Analysis citing Sacra described a revenue mix of roughly 38% marketplace and 62% private cloud, with private-cloud contracts averaging over $100 million against about $340,000 for marketplace customers. Whatever the exact split, the direction is clear and the company has followed it to its end: fewer, larger, longer contracts, until the marketplace disappeared from the website.

Against the rest of the table

Price comparison with the other profiled companies works only one layer at a time.

  • Against CoreWeave and Nebius, which sell GPU hours from chips they own, Fluidstack is not a price competitor in 2026; it has no public hours to sell. The comparison is on who can deliver a customer's gigawatt sooner, and on who carries the chip depreciation (they do; Fluidstack's customer does).
  • Against Crusoe, the closest model, the comparison is cost per delivered megawatt and months from ground-break to energisation. Neither company publishes either figure; Crusoe was reported raising at a $30 billion valuation in mid-2026 against Fluidstack's $18 billion.
  • Against the hyperscalers, the comparison Forbes draws is time: three months claimed against at least a year. A buyer paying a premium for speed is buying months of model training, and the value of a month at the frontier is what sets the ceiling on Fluidstack's margin.

For an engineer the practical point is that "what does Fluidstack cost" has no answer in dollars per GPU-hour. The answers are rent per megawatt-year (about $1.8 to $1.9 million, from the leases), all-in cost per gigawatt (tens of billions, with chips) and months to first token, and the last of these is the one the company sells.

Takeaway

  • The published rate card (H100 about $2.10, H200 about $2.30 per GPU-hour) is history; no public price exists in November 2026.
  • Landlord leases imply a market rent of about $1.8 to $1.9 million per MW-year for built, powered AI halls; chips and power, not buildings, dominate the all-in cost of about $50 billion per gigawatt.
  • Projected revenue lags contracted capacity by a year or more, which is the normal shape of a builder, not a sign of low prices.
  • Fluidstack competes on months to delivery rather than dollars per hour, and the value of a month at the frontier sets its margin.
