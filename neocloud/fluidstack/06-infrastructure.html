Infrastructure & Operations

Hardware generations, networking, cluster software, reliability and ratings.

Operating in other people's buildings

Through 2025 almost every Fluidstack deployment sat in a third-party data centre; SemiAnalysis wrote that "almost all of Fluidstack's customer deployments involve a third-party datacenter provider". The operating job was therefore the layer between a colocation landlord and a frontier lab: receive racks, image servers, bring up the fabric, run the scheduler, watch the fleet, replace what breaks, and keep the customer from ever needing to call the landlord. That is a harder job than running one's own building, because the operator controls neither the power nor the cooling, and it is the job the company was rated on.

The hardware was Nvidia: H100 and H200 SXM systems with InfiniBand, per aggregator listings, and GB200 NVL72 on request. No Fluidstack page confirms GB300 or any AMD part, and the company's own infrastructure pages have gone with the rest of the product site. Certifications (SOC 2, ISO 27001), uptime figures and SLAs are not published anywhere that could be read for this guide.

What ClusterMAX 2.0 found

In November 2025 SemiAnalysis published the second edition of ClusterMAX, its independent rating of GPU clouds, having benchmarked 84 providers and surveyed over 140 customers. Fluidstack debuted at Gold, one of six providers in the top two tiers and the only cloud to debut at Gold that round. The review is the single most useful public document about how the company operates, and it rewards reading as an engineer would.

  • Handover. Both Slurm and Kubernetes were usable within hours. The Slurm image had Pyxis and MPI in srun but lacked HPC-X until the team added it. The Kubernetes stack was full (Cilium, a ReadWriteMany CSI, GPU and Network Operators, node-problem-detector, kube-prometheus-stack, draino, ArgoCD).
  • Health checks. Heavy checks ran in the Slurm prolog at every job start, so an interactive job took over a minute to schedule. Passive DCGM checks were off, so a node with injected PCIe errors did not drain itself. The team put background checks on the roadmap.
  • Monitoring. Grafana was "solid" but missing NVLink Rx/Tx graphs, and two DCGM tensor-core metrics were wrong. Fixed the next day.
  • Security. The most serious finding: the cluster ran an nvidia-container-toolkit vulnerable to NVIDIAScape (CVE-2025-23266) and a GPU Operator a minor version behind. Both were patched quickly once raised. The reviewers noted the company was not in vendor embargo programmes and recommended automated dependency scanning and a formal security process.
  • Responsiveness. The reviewers' summary: "the speed with which the Fluidstack team addressed issues was unmatched in all our testing", and "issues that might take weeks or months to get addressed in a hyperscaler's ticketing system were fixed in hours". They called it the clearest example of a "Forward Deployed Engineering" ethos among the providers tested.

Read as a whole: a conventional, complete stack, shipped with the rough edges of a team moving fast, and an operating culture that closed every gap within a day. The Gold was for the culture. The security finding is the one an interviewer at a lab would raise, and the fair answer is that the fix was fast and the process was missing.

What ClusterMAX 3.0 changed

The third edition, published 23 September 2026, lists Fluidstack as "Unavailable". The reason given is not a failed test but a refusal to take one: the edition groups Fluidstack with providers "who now claim to have zero spare capacity and/or refuse to test with us", and adds that "Fluidstack has (currently) exited the managed clusters market, as far as we are concerned, because their focus has shifted from managed Nvidia clusters to bare-metal TPU deployments at 100K+ chip scale". The same edition put CoreWeave and Nebius at Platinum, Google Cloud and Oracle at Gold, Azure, Lambda, Firmus and TensorWave at Silver, and Crusoe and Together AI at Bronze.

Two things should be said plainly. First, "Unavailable" is not a downgrade on quality; it means there was nothing to test. Second, Forbes reported that SemiAnalysis' founder, Dylan Patel, holds a stake in Fluidstack through a vehicle estimated at about $140 million, and that a dispute between SemiAnalysis and a former employee, who alleged pressure to include Fluidstack TPU data, is in arbitration. Those facts are on the record; no public source connects them to either rating, and this guide does not.

The TPU turn

The one sentence in ClusterMAX 3.0 explains the operating company of 2026 better than anything Fluidstack has published. Anthropic agreed in October 2025 to use up to a million Google TPUs; the Fluidstack-operated campuses are where a large share of them land; and a TPU pod at 100,000-chip scale is not a managed Slurm cluster with a kubeconfig in the UI. It is bare metal, delivered as halls, with Google's own software stack on the chips and the operator responsible for power, cooling, networking, hardware lifecycle and the physical work of bringing tens of thousands of hosts online on a schedule written into a lease.

That shift moves the company's engineering centre of gravity. The 248 open roles in November 2026 lean to physical engineering, manufacturing, supply chain and construction, and the website's fifth challenge is to "operate tens of millions of XPUs at high reliability", a vendor-neutral word that covers TPUs. The forward-deployed habit that SemiAnalysis praised is still the asset, applied now to a single customer's fleet rather than to many customers' clusters.

Building and operating at campus scale

What is public about the build method is in chapter 03: prefabricated modules from a factory, robot cells for welding and painting, closed-loop cooling with a single fill, and targets of a data centre in three months or 250 MW in six. The operating method at campus scale is less visible, but the leases contain its constraints. Rent is payable on the landlord's schedule; the Google backstop, by one report, falls away if construction slips more than 180 days, which gives Fluidstack an exit but also a hard date; and triple-net terms put every pass-through cost, from power to maintenance, on the tenant.

Fitch's two flags on the Indiana bond, limited data-centre experience and no guaranteed maximum price, are the operating risks stated by an outsider with money at stake. The company's answers in public are hires (more than a dozen former Tesla and SpaceX staff, utility-scale solar developers) and the Glendale factory. The answers in evidence will be the first halls: Barber Lake in late 2026, River Bend and Indiana in 2027.

✓ Check yourself

Answer from memory before opening each one.

  1. ClusterMAX 2.0 found a known CVE on the cluster and awarded Gold anyway. What did the rating reward, and what did it mark down?

    Show answer

    It rewarded a complete, usable stack and a team that fixed every finding within hours or a day. It marked down the absence of process: no vendor embargo membership, no automated dependency scanning. See what ClusterMAX 2.0 found.

  2. Is "Unavailable" in ClusterMAX 3.0 a downgrade? What is the stated reason?

    Show answer

    No. It means there was no managed cluster to test; the stated reason is zero spare capacity and a shift to bare-metal TPU deployments at 100,000-plus chip scale. See what ClusterMAX 3.0 changed.

  3. How does a bare-metal TPU campus change the operator's job compared with a managed Nvidia cluster?

    Show answer

    The scheduler and the software on the chips belong to Google and the customer; the operator owns power, cooling, networking, hardware lifecycle and the schedule for bringing tens of thousands of hosts online under a lease. The centre of gravity moves from cluster software to physical operations. See the TPU turn.

  4. Which two clauses in the leases turn construction speed from a marketing claim into an operating constraint?

    Show answer

    Rent payable on the landlord's schedule regardless of chip arrival, and the reported 180-day delay threshold on the Google backstop. Together with triple-net pass-throughs they make every month of slip a cost. See building and operating at campus scale.

Takeaway

  • Through 2025 Fluidstack operated Nvidia clusters inside other companies' buildings; ClusterMAX 2.0 rated that work Gold for a complete stack and unmatched speed of fixes, while noting a CVE and missing security process.
  • ClusterMAX 3.0 lists it as Unavailable because there was nothing to test: capacity is committed and the focus is bare-metal TPU deployments at 100,000-plus chip scale.
  • The operator's job has moved from cluster software to physical operations at campus scale, which the hiring mix confirms.
  • No published SLA, uptime or certification exists; the first delivered halls in late 2026 and 2027 are the evidence to wait for.
