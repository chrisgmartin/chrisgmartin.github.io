Add the figures and the contracted critical IT load is roughly 1.4 GW on the leased campuses, before Cameron County and Virginia, which matches Forbes' report that the company expected to manage up to 1.3 GW across more than ten sites in 2026. The Anthropic announcement named only New York and Texas, "with more sites to come"; Data Center Dynamics' reading, which the later water-fund and Hut 8 announcements support, is that Lake Mariner is the New York site and Barber Lake and Abernathy are the Texas ones.

Leased campuses

Three of the four leased campuses belong to former bitcoin miners, and that is not a coincidence. TeraWulf, Cipher and Hut 8 each held what a GPU builder lacks in 2025: an energised site with hundreds of megawatts of interconnection already approved, transformers on order or in place, and a regulator used to them. Converting a mining hall to a liquid-cooled AI hall is a rebuild, but the grid connection is the asset that takes years, and the miners had it. Crusoe made the same move from the other side, as a miner that became a cloud.

The deals share a template. The landlord borrows against the lease (Cipher's 7.125% notes due 2030, Hut 8's loan from J.P. Morgan and Goldman Sachs at up to 85% of cost, TeraWulf's project financing); Fluidstack signs as tenant; Google guarantees Fluidstack's payments for the base term and receives warrants in the landlord (about 14% of TeraWulf pro forma, about 5.4% of Cipher). Chapter 04 explains why each party accepts that structure.

Indiana is the fourth and the largest single site. Here Fluidstack is a co-owner through the Meridian Arc joint venture with Next Frontier rather than a plain tenant, and the financing came from the high-yield bond market directly: $5.7 billion of five-year notes, which the trade press called the largest AI-driven high-yield deal to date, rated BB by Fitch on the strength of the Google guarantee, with debt at about 92% of project cost and about $505 million of sponsor equity. Fitch also flagged the sponsors' limited data-centre experience and the lack of a guaranteed maximum construction price. Those two flags describe Fluidstack's risk profile as well as any sentence in this guide.

Campuses it builds itself

Cameron County, Texas, announced on 21 September 2026, is the first campus Fluidstack presents as its own: a $4 billion first phase in the Rio Grande Valley planned for up to 1.5 GW, more than 2,500 construction jobs, and a set of commitments written for a county that had passed a resolution on data centres. The company says it requested and received no property-tax abatements, will pay for its own energy, infrastructure and water, will limit sound at the property line to 60 decibels, and will take part in Public Utility Commission of Texas and ERCOT audits. The cooling loop is closed, with a tank "less water than an Olympic-sized swimming pool", supplied by a non-municipal water corporation.

Wythe County, Virginia, followed on 10 November 2026: a 99-acre campus at Progress Park, first proposed in December 2025, over $1 billion of investment, about 1,000 construction jobs and 75 permanent ones, a closed loop with a 400,000-gallon initial fill, and the company covering power-infrastructure upgrades. The county expects $10 million a year in revenue by 2028. No building count or power figure has been given.

Both announcements read as a response to the politics of 2026 as much as to engineering. Power, water and tax terms are the issues on which counties have rejected projects, and Fluidstack's community page leads with them. The Colorado City water fund of September 2026, $10 million from Fluidstack, Cipher and Anthropic for a town next to Barber Lake that had lost water for four days, is the same move at an existing site.

Projects it left

In February 2025, at the Paris AI Action Summit, Fluidstack signed a memorandum with the French government for a 1 GW AI campus in northern France, with a first phase of about €10 billion to be operational in 2026 and nuclear power behind it. In March 2025 it announced, with the developer Eclairion, a 40 MW site at Bruyères-le-Châtel in Essonne, described as an 18,000-GPU supercomputer for Mistral, with expansion to over 100 MW. Both were the company's largest commitments at the time.

On 18 March 2026 Bloomberg reported that Fluidstack had withdrawn from the French 1 GW project to prioritise the United States, and that it had exited the Essonne project as well; Mistral was reported to be proceeding without it. Neither Fluidstack nor the French government has published a statement that could be read for this guide, so the withdrawal rests on that reporting. The timing is consistent with everything else here: the Anthropic programme was announced in November 2025, the Series A closed in January 2026, and the headquarters had just moved to New York.

How it builds

The company's claims about construction speed are the core of its pitch and should be quoted precisely. The website's list of challenges includes "build 250MW data centers in six months from breaking ground". Forbes reported the claim that it can "build a data center in just three months" against at least a year for a hyperscaler. SiliconANGLE, reading job postings, described factory-built prefabricated modules with robot cells for welding and painting parts, which is the same approach Crusoe uses. The Glendale warehouses, which the landlord said would be used for assembly, manufacturing and possibly research rather than as a data centre, are where that factory appears to be.

Two other items from the challenges list show where the company thinks the bottleneck moves next: "deploy hundreds of gigawatts of solar", which Forbes connected to hiring utility-scale solar developers and to state grid limits, and "onshore manufacturing for all compute & power infrastructure". The Next Platform's report that electricians, not GPUs, are the constraint in 2026 is the backdrop. None of the three-month or six-month claims has yet been demonstrated on a campus with public dates: the first halls at River Bend and Indiana are due in 2027, and Barber Lake's were reported for late 2026. That is the thing to watch.

✓ Check yourself

Answer from memory before opening each one.

  1. Why are three of the four landlords former bitcoin miners, and what exactly did Fluidstack buy from them?

    Show answer

    An energised site with hundreds of megawatts of approved interconnection, the asset that takes years to obtain. The halls are rebuilt; the grid connection is reused. See leased campuses.

  2. Fitch rated the Indiana notes BB and raised two flags. What were they, and why do they matter beyond that one bond?

    Show answer

    Limited data-centre experience of the sponsors and no guaranteed maximum construction price. Both describe Fluidstack as a builder in general, and the BB rating itself rests on Google's guarantee rather than on Fluidstack. See leased campuses.

  3. What do the Cameron County commitments (no abatements, own water, 60 dB, ERCOT audits) tell you about what slows a campus down in 2026?

    Show answer

    Local consent over power, water and tax, not equipment. The company front-loads those terms because counties have rejected projects on them. See campuses it builds itself.

  4. The company claims a data centre in three months and 250 MW in six. What is the honest status of those claims, and when can they be checked?

    Show answer

    They are targets, not demonstrated results on a campus with public dates. Barber Lake's halls were reported for late 2026 and the first River Bend and Indiana halls are due in 2027, so 2027 is when the claims meet evidence. See how it builds.

Takeaway

  • Roughly 1.4 GW of contracted critical IT load across four leased campuses in New York, Texas, Louisiana and Indiana, plus two campuses of its own in Texas (up to 1.5 GW planned) and Virginia.
  • Three landlords are former miners whose grid connections were the real purchase; Indiana is a joint venture financed by a $5.7 billion high-yield bond rated on Google's guarantee.
  • France, the company's biggest 2025 commitment, was dropped in March 2026 to focus on the United States.
  • The speed claims are three months per data centre and 250 MW in six; the first public tests arrive in late 2026 and 2027.
