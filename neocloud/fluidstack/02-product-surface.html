Section B · What it sells

Product Surface

Dedicated clusters, the software layer, contract terms, what it does not sell.

What it sells

As of November 2026 the public website has no product page and no pricing page: the navigation is Mission, Challenges, Community, News and Jobs, and the old /pricing path returns a 404. That is itself the clearest statement of the product. Fluidstack sells one thing, to a handful of buyers, under contract: delivered, operated compute capacity at a named site, measured in megawatts of critical IT load and years of term.

The deliverable has three parts, and the company's announcements and its landlords' filings let you see each one.

  • The site and the power. Either a lease on a landlord's campus (TeraWulf's Lake Mariner in New York, Cipher's Barber Lake in Texas, Hut 8's River Bend in Louisiana, the Meridian Arc joint venture in Indiana) or a campus the company builds itself (Cameron County in Texas, Wythe County in Virginia). Chapter 03 lists them.
  • The fit-out. Data halls designed around the customer's chips. Anthropic's announcement says the New York and Texas facilities are "custom built for Anthropic, with a focus on maximizing efficiency for their workloads". SemiAnalysis reported in September 2026 that the focus had moved to "bare-metal TPU deployments at 100K+ chip scale", which fits Anthropic's agreement with Google for up to a million TPUs.
  • Operations. Fluidstack runs the clusters. The Hut 8 partnership describes "clusters operated by Fluidstack" for Anthropic, with a separate operations services agreement that Google also backstops.

The software layer

The only detailed public look at the software is SemiAnalysis' ClusterMAX 2.0 review of November 2025, which tested a managed Nvidia cluster. The stack it describes is conventional and complete: Slurm with Pyxis and MPI integrated into srun; Kubernetes with Cilium, a CSI driver with ReadWriteMany volumes, the Nvidia GPU and Network Operators, node-problem-detector, kube-prometheus-stack, draino and ArgoCD, with a kubeconfig downloadable from the UI; Grafana dashboards; and health checks run from a Slurm prolog. Third-party research names the provisioning layer "Atlas" (bare-metal and KVM instances) and the monitoring layer "Lighthouse", but no Fluidstack page confirms those names, so treat them as labels, not products.

The review also found the gaps you would expect from a fast-moving team: the prolog health checks took over a minute per job start, background DCGM checks were off so a node with injected PCIe errors did not drain itself, HPC-X was missing from the base image, two Grafana metrics were wrong, and the container toolkit was a version behind on a known CVE. Every item was fixed in hours or put on the roadmap the same day, which is what earned the Gold rating. Chapter 06 covers the review in full. For a buyer the lesson is that the software is a means of operating the customer's hardware, not a platform the customer logs in to and self-serves.

What it no longer sells

Three earlier products are gone or going.

  • On-demand GPU hours. Aggregators still carry list prices from the old pricing page (H100 at $2.10 and H200 at $2.30 per GPU-hour, GB200 NVL72 "on request"). The page no longer exists, and a comparison site noted in September 2026 that Fluidstack publishes no prices. Chapter 05 explains what replaced them.
  • Managed Nvidia clusters for new customers. SemiAnalysis moved Fluidstack to "Unavailable" in ClusterMAX 3.0 (September 2026) because the company "claim[ed] to have zero spare capacity and/or refuse[d] to test", and concluded it had exited the managed-clusters market for now. Existing managed customers are not named.
  • Inference. Unlike Together AI or Hyperbolic, Fluidstack never built a per-token API and sells none now.

The company's own boilerplate still calls it "the leading AI cloud platform trusted by the world's top labs", and the ClusterMAX page still describes it as an AI cloud. Both are true of the company that existed in 2024. The 2026 company is closer to a developer-operator, and candidates and buyers should use that frame.

Terms and commitments

Fluidstack's own contracts with customers are private. Its obligations as a tenant are public because its landlords are listed, and they set the floor for what any customer must have committed. The pattern, from the TeraWulf, Cipher, Hut 8 and Meridian Arc filings:

  • Ten- to fifteen-year initial terms with five-year renewal options, triple-net, with annual escalators (3.0% at River Bend).
  • Capacity in blocks of roughly 160 to 430 MW of critical IT load per campus, delivered by data hall on a schedule written into the lease.
  • Google guaranteeing the lease payments and pass-through costs for the base term, and taking warrants or fees in return.
  • Rights of first offer on expansion power at the same campus (up to 1,000 MW at River Bend).

A customer therefore signs for years, not months, and for halls, not racks. The minimum viable Fluidstack deal in 2026 is a frontier lab or a hyperscaler-class buyer with a credit partner. No published minimum exists below that because nothing below that is on offer.

Who the buyer is

Anthropic is the named anchor: the $50 billion programme in New York and Texas, the Hut 8 partnership of "at least 245 MW and up to 2.295 GW", and the Barber Lake water-fund announcement in which Anthropic appears as the tenant beside Cipher and Fluidstack. Forbes names Meta, Jane Street and Black Forest Labs as other current customers, and Macquarie's page lists Meta, Poolside and Black Forest Labs as companies that trust the platform. None of those deals has a public size. The French government and Mistral were customers in 2025 and are not in 2026 (chapter 07).

Two roles sit beside the customer. Google is partner, guarantor and shareholder at once, and its interest is selling TPUs. The landlords, three former bitcoin miners and a developer joint venture, are customers of a kind too: they buy Fluidstack's ability to turn a powered shell into a tenanted, operated campus, which is why SemiAnalysis called the company a hired gun.

✓ Check yourself

Answer from memory before opening each one.

  1. The website has no product or pricing page. What does that tell you about the product, and what would a product page have to show if one existed?

    Show answer

    That the product is sold under multi-year contract to a few buyers, in megawatts and halls, not in GPU hours to anyone with a card. A page would have to show sites, power, delivery dates and operating terms, which are negotiated, not listed. See what it sells.

  2. Name the three parts of the deliverable and say which one Google's backstop attaches to.

    Show answer

    Site and power, fit-out, and operations. The backstop covers the lease payments and pass-through costs to the landlord, and at River Bend a separate backstop covers the operations services agreement. See what it sells and terms.

  3. A colleague says Fluidstack's software stack is a differentiator because of the Slurm and Kubernetes work in the ClusterMAX review. What is the better reading?

    Show answer

    The stack is conventional and had the usual gaps; what the reviewers praised was how fast the team fixed them. The software is how Fluidstack operates a customer's hardware, and the differentiator is the operating team and its speed. See the software layer.

  4. Why would a company drop a Gold-rated managed-cluster product within a year of winning the rating?

    Show answer

    Because one customer's programme absorbed all its capacity and attention, and that programme runs on TPUs at a scale where managed Nvidia clusters for other buyers no longer fit. SemiAnalysis' note about zero spare capacity says as much. See what it no longer sells.

Takeaway

  • The product in November 2026 is delivered, operated capacity at a named site, sold in megawatts and years to a few buyers; the website has no product or pricing page.
  • The deliverable is site and power, custom fit-out and operations, with the software a means of running the customer's chips rather than a self-serve platform.
  • On-demand GPU hours, managed Nvidia clusters for new customers and inference are gone or paused; Anthropic's TPU programme is where the capacity went.
  • Terms mirror the landlord leases: ten to fifteen years, triple-net, escalators, Google guarantees, rights of first offer on more power.
