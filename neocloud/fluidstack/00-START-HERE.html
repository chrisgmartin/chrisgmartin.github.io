Where Fluidstack sits: a builder of power and sites, not a GPU reseller.

Scope & audience

Eleven chapters on Fluidstack: the Oxford-founded GPU renter that became a builder of gigawatt campuses for Anthropic, how it pays for them with leases that Google guarantees, what it sells and no longer sells, who its landlords and customers are, and where it stands against CoreWeave, Nebius and Crusoe. Written for engineers and operators preparing to work with, sell to or interview at the company. Facts are as of November 2026 and each chapter names its sources; the company discloses little itself, so several figures come from counterparties' filings and from Forbes' reporting on an investor memo.

Key framings to carry

  1. Fluidstack is a builder and operator, not a chip owner. CoreWeave and Nebius buy GPUs and rent them. Fluidstack leases or builds the shell, secures the power, and runs clusters of chips that customers increasingly bring themselves. The balance sheet, the risks and the margins all follow from that choice.
  2. Credit comes from Google, not from Fluidstack. Every large lease since August 2025 carries a Google backstop of Fluidstack's obligations, and Google takes warrants in the landlord in return. Rating agencies rate the landlords' bonds on Google's promise, because Fluidstack itself is unrated. Read every deal with that in view.
  3. Speed is the product. The company's pitch to Anthropic, to landlords and to recruits is the same sentence: build faster than anyone else. The claims (a data centre in about three months, 250 MW in six months from breaking ground) are the thing to test, and the history of the category says the test is 2027, when the first halls are due.
