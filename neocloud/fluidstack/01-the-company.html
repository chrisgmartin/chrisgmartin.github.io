Section A · Origin

The Company

Oxford founding in 2017, the GPU-aggregation years, the pivot to building, leadership today.

Founding (2017)

Fluidstack was founded in London in 2017 by Gary Wu, Jamie Cox and César Maklary. Wu and Cox met at Oxford, where Wu read economics and Cox read classics before dropping out to take a Thiel Fellowship. Maklary came from aerodynamics work for a Formula One team and joined in 2020. One directory lists 2018 as the founding year; Forbes, which interviewed the founders in September 2026, says 2017.

The first business was the smallest possible version of a GPU cloud: renting gamers' idle graphics cards to AI researchers who could not get time on university clusters. It was a marketplace with no owned hardware and no data centre, which is the same starting point as Vast.ai, and it taught the company the two things it still trades on, finding capacity other people are not using and running someone else's hardware well enough that a customer does not care whose it is.

Founders

  • Gary Wu: co-founder and CEO. The public voice of the company in the Anthropic, Hut 8 and headquarters announcements. Forbes gave his age as 31 in September 2026.
  • César Maklary: co-founder and President. Quoted on the ClusterMAX Gold award in November 2025. One older directory still lists him as CEO, which is stale.
  • Jamie Cox: co-founder. His current title is not stated in any primary source read for this guide.

Forbes reported that the three became billionaires on paper at the September 2026 valuation. The detail that matters more for an interview is the age of the team: founders under 32 running a company that has signed more than $20 billion of lease commitments, which explains both the pace and the dependence on older partners for credit (chapter 04).

Three pivots

The company's shape changed three times, and each change moved it one layer down the stack.

  1. Marketplace to wholesale renter (2020 to 2022). During the pandemic Fluidstack stopped aggregating consumer cards and started renting blocks of GPUs from corporate and university labs, then subletting them to AI start-ups. Forbes names Poolside, Mistral and Character.ai as customers from this period. The company still sold GPU hours, but now on hardware it controlled for the length of a contract.
  2. Renter to managed-cluster operator (2023 to 2025). As frontier labs wanted thousands of interconnected GPUs rather than hundreds, Fluidstack began running whole clusters inside other companies' data centres: it imaged the servers, ran Slurm or Kubernetes, monitored and fixed them, and the customer saw one cluster. SemiAnalysis later described this model as "the hired gun that organizations go to in order to turn bronze-tier datacenter infrastructure into a gold-tier customer experience". The Macquarie debt facility of 2025, secured on GPUs deployed in an Icelandic geothermal-powered site for "a leading AI research lab", belongs to this phase.
  3. Operator to builder (2025 onward). From August 2025 the company signed leases on whole campuses with former bitcoin miners, then began building its own, and Anthropic chose it in November 2025 for a $50 billion programme of custom sites in New York and Texas. Forbes' framing is the one to remember: CoreWeave and Nebius buy chips and rent them out; Fluidstack builds the building, secures the power and hosts the customer's chips, which it believes lets it move faster.

Each pivot kept the previous layer alive for a while. SemiAnalysis tested a managed Nvidia cluster in late 2025 and awarded it Gold; by September 2026 it reported that Fluidstack had "exited the managed clusters market, as far as we are concerned", with its focus "shifted from managed Nvidia clusters to bare-metal TPU deployments at 100K+ chip scale". Chapter 06 takes that up.

Leadership and offices

In February 2025 the company announced a layer of operating executives: Rob Perdue as COO (from The Trade Desk), Katherine Ollerhead as General Counsel, Mike McDonald as VP Product and Dan Carpenter as VP Sales. The announcement page could not be loaded for this guide, so the names rest on contemporaneous reporting.

On 4 December 2025 Fluidstack moved its global headquarters from London to Midtown Manhattan, citing "over 1,000 jobs coming to New York" through the New York data-centre project, and said it would keep growing in Austin and San Francisco in 2026. Forbes reported that it had hired more than a dozen former Tesla and SpaceX staff in the year to September 2026, plus engineers with utility-scale solar experience. The careers page in November 2026 listed 248 open roles, with the largest teams being Physical Engineering & Design (49), Software & Automation (47), Manufacturing (26) and Supply Chain & Logistics (22): a hardware and construction company with a software team, not the other way round.

No official headcount exists. Directory figures range from 30 to 500 and are all undated; treat any number as a guess.

Scale today

  • Revenue. Over $66 million in 2024, the last year with UK filings. An investor memo seen by Forbes put 2025 at about $200 million and projected $660 million for 2026. The company has not confirmed either figure.
  • Power. The same memo said the company was on track to manage up to 1.3 GW across more than ten sites in 2026 and aspired to more than 17 GW by 2030. The website's own ambition is "100s of GW deployed over the next five years".
  • Equity. An $830 million Series A at a $7.5 billion valuation, closed in January 2026 and announced in July, led by Situational Awareness; then a reported $1.5 billion round led by Jane Street at over $18 billion in September 2026, which the company has not announced. Chapter 09 has the full table.
  • Debt. Over $15 billion across the projects, by Forbes' reading of SEC filings, nearly all of it borrowed by landlords and joint ventures against leases that Google guarantees.

In short: a company whose own revenue is in the hundreds of millions, sitting at the centre of commitments in the tens of billions. That gap is the subject of the rest of this guide.

✓ Check yourself

Answer from memory before opening each one.

  1. Fluidstack started as a marketplace for idle gaming GPUs. Which habit from that business survives in the current one, and which was dropped?

    Show answer

    Running other people's hardware well enough that the customer does not care whose it is survives, as operating clusters in landlords' buildings. Owning no hardware and no building was dropped once frontier labs needed whole campuses. See three pivots.

  2. Why does Forbes' one-line contrast with CoreWeave and Nebius matter more than any valuation figure?

    Show answer

    It defines the balance sheet. A chip owner carries depreciating GPUs and sells hours; a builder carries leases and construction risk and hosts the customer's chips. The risks, the margins and the role of Google's guarantees all follow from that distinction. See three pivots.

  3. The careers page lists 248 roles. What do the team sizes tell you about what kind of company this is in 2026?

    Show answer

    Physical engineering, manufacturing, supply chain and construction outnumber software. It is an infrastructure builder with a software team, so an engineer joining should expect to serve procurement, construction and operations, not a cloud console. See leadership and offices.

  4. Revenue of about $200 million against more than $15 billion of project debt. Why is that not the contradiction it looks like?

    Show answer

    The debt is borrowed by landlords and joint ventures against long leases, and Google guarantees the lease payments, so lenders are underwriting Google's credit and the customer's demand, not Fluidstack's income statement. See scale today and chapter 04.

Takeaway

  • Founded in London in 2017 by three Oxford-connected founders; headquartered in New York since December 2025, with Austin and San Francisco growing.
  • Three pivots, each one layer down the stack: marketplace, wholesale renter, managed-cluster operator, and now builder of campuses that host customers' chips.
  • Revenue in the hundreds of millions, equity valuation reported above $18 billion, and more than $15 billion of project debt carried by landlords on Google's credit.
  • The hiring mix in 2026 is physical engineering, manufacturing and supply chain first, software second.
