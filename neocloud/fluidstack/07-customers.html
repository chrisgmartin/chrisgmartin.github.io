Section D · How it runs

Customers

Mistral, Anthropic and the frontier labs: who buys, what they buy, and for how long.

The anchor: Anthropic

On 12 November 2025 Anthropic announced a $50 billion investment in American computing infrastructure, with Fluidstack building custom data centres in Texas and New York, sites coming online through 2026, about 800 permanent and 2,400 construction jobs, and more sites to come. Anthropic's stated reason for the choice was Fluidstack's "ability to move with exceptional agility, enabling rapid delivery of gigawatts of power". Forbes' gloss is that $50 billion is roughly the cost of a gigawatt with chips, and that the programme supports Anthropic's October 2025 agreement to use up to a million Google TPUs. One analyst has cautioned that the headline figure is not all revenue to Fluidstack, which is right: it is the cost of the programme, of which rent and operations are a part.

The relationship widened within weeks. On 17 December 2025 Hut 8 announced a partnership with Anthropic and Fluidstack to deliver "at least 245 MW and up to 2.295 GW" of capacity for Anthropic, "leveraging clusters operated by Fluidstack", starting at River Bend in Louisiana. Anthropic's head of compute, James Bradbury, called it "an expansion of our existing work with Fluidstack". In September 2026 the Colorado City water fund named Anthropic beside Fluidstack and Cipher at Barber Lake, which confirms the Texas tenant. By any public measure Anthropic is most of the business.

The other names

Forbes names three other current customers: Meta, Jane Street and Black Forest Labs. Macquarie's page says the platform is "trusted by Meta, Poolside, and Black Forest Labs", and describes the GPUs it financed as serving "a leading AI research lab" in Iceland. A job posting lists Anthropic, Meta, Mistral and Poolside as customers. No size, term or site is public for any of these, and Meta's presence may be as small as a managed cluster from the 2024 era. Jane Street is both a customer and, since September 2026, the lead investor in the latest round, which is a pattern worth noting: a trading firm with large compute needs taking equity in its supplier.

From the wholesale-renter years Forbes also names Poolside, Mistral and Character.ai. Of these, Poolside still appears on current lists; Character.ai does not appear in any 2026 source; and Mistral is discussed below. The honest summary is that beyond Anthropic the customer list is a set of names without numbers, and that the company's choice to commit its capacity to one programme means the list is unlikely to grow in 2026.

Former customers

France was the showcase customer of 2025. The February 2025 memorandum with the French state for a 1 GW campus, and the March 2025 agreement with Eclairion for a 40 MW, 18,000-GPU site for Mistral at Bruyères-le-Châtel, made Fluidstack the named builder of Europe's sovereign AI capacity. In March 2026 Bloomberg reported that it had withdrawn from both to prioritise the United States, with Mistral reported to be continuing on its own. No Fluidstack statement on the withdrawal has been found.

Two readings are possible and both are probably true. The company could not staff a European gigawatt and an American one at the same time with founders in their thirties and a few hundred people, and chose the customer with a credit partner. And the economics of a sovereign project, with state involvement and a 2026 operational date promised in early 2025, were harder than a Texas lease with a Google guarantee. For a European buyer the lesson is that Fluidstack in 2026 is an American builder.

Google, in three roles

Google is not a customer in the ordinary sense, but it is the counterparty that makes the customers possible. It guarantees the leases (chapter 04). It supplies the chips, since Anthropic's programme runs on TPUs and SemiAnalysis describes the deployments as bare-metal TPU at 100,000-plus chip scale. And it is reportedly a shareholder in Fluidstack, alongside being a roughly 14% shareholder in Anthropic. Forbes' headline framed the whole company as "helping Google take on Nvidia", which overstates a builder's role in a chip war but captures why Google's money is there.

The dependency runs both ways to a point. Google could guarantee another operator's leases; it has not, publicly, and the warrants it holds in TeraWulf and Cipher are tied to Fluidstack's tenancies. Fluidstack could operate Nvidia campuses for other labs; it has told SemiAnalysis, in effect, that it has no capacity to. For now each is the other's chosen instrument.

What concentration means here

Customer concentration is the first risk any reader will name, and it is real: one customer, one chip supplier who is also the guarantor, and leases that run ten to fifteen years. Three facts qualify it. First, Anthropic's demand is itself diversified across suppliers; in September 2026 Forbes counted about $75 billion of deals with Nscale and Lambda in a single week, plus Amazon, Google Cloud and Musk-linked capacity, so Fluidstack is one of several builders, not the only one. Second, Anthropic has begun contracting with landlords directly, as in the twenty-year, $19 billion Justified campus lease with TeraWulf in July 2026 that does not mention Fluidstack. Third, the leases are on buildings in Texas, New York, Louisiana and Indiana with grid connections, which have other possible tenants if the first one leaves.

The first fact lowers the risk that Anthropic fails; the second raises the risk that Anthropic stops needing an intermediary; the third limits the damage either way. An interviewee asked about concentration should give all three rather than the word alone.

✓ Check yourself

Answer from memory before opening each one.

  1. Is the $50 billion Anthropic programme Fluidstack revenue? What is it, and what share of it does an operator plausibly see?

    Show answer

    It is the cost of about a gigawatt including chips. Rent per megawatt-year is under $2 million against roughly $50 billion per gigawatt all-in, so the building and operating share is small; the chips and power are most of it. See the anchor and chapter 05.

  2. Which three pieces of public evidence place Anthropic at Fluidstack's Texas and Louisiana sites?

    Show answer

    The Hut 8 partnership "for Anthropic, leveraging clusters operated by Fluidstack" at River Bend; the Colorado City water fund naming Anthropic beside Cipher at Barber Lake; and Data Center Dynamics' reading of the November 2025 announcement. See the anchor.

  3. Why did the France withdrawal happen when it did, and what does it tell a European buyer?

    Show answer

    It followed the Anthropic programme, the Series A and the New York headquarters move within four months; the company chose the customer with a credit partner over a sovereign project with a hard 2026 date. A European buyer should treat Fluidstack as an American builder in 2026. See former customers.

  4. Give the three qualifications to the concentration risk and say which one cuts against Fluidstack rather than for it.

    Show answer

    Anthropic's demand is spread across many suppliers (lowers failure risk); Anthropic now leases directly from landlords (raises disintermediation risk, against Fluidstack); the collateral is powered buildings with other possible tenants (limits damage). See what concentration means here.

Takeaway

  • Anthropic is the anchor: the $50 billion New York and Texas programme, the Hut 8 partnership up to 2.295 GW, and the Barber Lake tenancy, all on Google TPUs.
  • Meta, Jane Street, Black Forest Labs and Poolside are named without numbers; Jane Street is also the lead investor.
  • France and Mistral were the 2025 showcase and were dropped in March 2026 for the US programme.
  • Google is guarantor, chip supplier and reported shareholder at once; concentration is real but qualified by Anthropic's many suppliers, its direct leases, and real-estate collateral.
