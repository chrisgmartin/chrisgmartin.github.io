Section C · Operations

Infrastructure

Lambda's infrastructure footprint is meaningful but smaller than CoreWeave's. Multiple regions, current-generation GPUs, InfiniBand fabric for clusters. Operationally sound rather than headline-grabbing.

Datacenter footprint

As of November 2026 Lambda lists cloud regions in the US (Virginia, Washington DC, Illinois, Texas, California, Arizona and Utah), Japan (Tokyo and Osaka), India, Germany and Israel, and not every instance type is in every region. Its own site also names sites in Southern California, Kansas City (a planned 100MW site with more than 10,000 NVIDIA GPUs), Chicago, Atlanta, Dallas-Fort Worth and Mountain View. Capacity has grown substantially through 2023-2026 as funding has supported aggressive buildouts. New regional sites continue to come online.

Total capacity is meaningful but doesn't approach CoreWeave's scale. Lambda is comfortable with this — the customer base doesn't require multi-hundred-MW campus capacity that the largest reservations need.

GPU mix

Lambda's GPU mix:

  • A100 80GB (substantial legacy fleet from earlier deployments).
  • H100 80GB (the bulk of current-generation deployment).
  • H200 is not on the current price list or in the on-demand docs.
  • B200 (coming online with allocation availability).
  • No L40S is listed. The docs list GH200, A10, A6000, V100 and RTX 6000 as smaller options, and GB300 NVL72 and HGX B300 appear on the Superclusters page.

Consumer cards aren't part of Lambda Cloud — the cloud product is datacenter-grade only. Workstation hardware covered that market until Lambda ended the hardware business in August 2025.

Network

Within clusters:

  • NVLink + NVSwitch for in-node GPU communication.
  • InfiniBand HDR / NDR for inter-node communication in cluster configurations.
  • Standard datacenter Ethernet for control plane.

The fabric is sufficient for cluster sizes Lambda targets — tens to hundreds of GPUs in a training cluster. In 2025 very large clusters (thousands of GPUs) went to CoreWeave or Crusoe. Lambda's Superclusters page now quotes single-tenant clusters from 4,000 to 165,000+ GPUs and names no customer at that size.

Operations

Lambda's operational team includes:

  • Datacenter operations across sites.
  • Network engineering.
  • Customer success / technical account management for larger deployments.
  • Engineering teams maintaining the platform software.

The operational quality is enterprise-grade. Multi-year customer relationships demonstrate this.

Scaling

Lambda's capacity scaling is driven by customer demand and capital availability. The pace through 2024-2026 has been aggressive, with new regions and capacity additions in step with funding rounds. Not as fast as CoreWeave's growth but meaningful.

✓ Check yourself

Answer from memory before opening each one.

  1. Why do cluster configurations use both NVLink with NVSwitch and InfiniBand?

    Show answer

    NVLink and NVSwitch link GPUs inside a node, and InfiniBand links nodes. Distributed training's all-reduce spans both levels, so each carries part of the traffic. See network.

  2. Where does Lambda's fabric stop being enough, and where do those customers go?

    Show answer

    1-Click Clusters run 16 to 512 GPUs. In 2025 runs of thousands of GPUs went to CoreWeave or Crusoe, and Lambda now quotes Superclusters from 4,000 GPUs. See network.

  3. Why are there no consumer cards in Lambda Cloud, and where do they live?

    Show answer

    The cloud product is datacenter-grade only. Consumer-card demand was served by the workstation business until Lambda ended it in August 2025. See GPU mix.

  4. A customer needs a 4,000-GPU training run next quarter. What does the infrastructure chapter tell you?

    Show answer

    Until late 2025 Lambda's capacity, fabric and capital were sized for mid-market clusters. The Microsoft agreement for tens of thousands of GPUs and the 2026 debt facilities show it can now build at that scale for an anchor customer, but a 4,000-GPU block next quarter still depends on what is already contracted. Compare with CoreWeave or Crusoe and ask Lambda for evidence on any capacity it commits. See datacenter footprint and scaling.

  5. Capacity grows in step with funding rounds. Is that a strength or a constraint?

    Show answer

    It is disciplined, because capacity follows capital and demand. It is a constraint when a customer needs a large block sooner than the next round can pay for. See scaling.

Takeaway

  • Lambda lists cloud regions in the US, Japan, India, Germany and Israel, with capacity growing since 2023 as funding allowed.
  • The listed fleet spans A100, H100 and B200, plus GH200 and smaller cards. H200 and L40S are not listed as of November 2026.
  • Consumer cards are not part of the cloud product, which is datacenter-grade only.
  • NVLink and NVSwitch connect GPUs within a node and InfiniBand connects nodes in a cluster.
  • The fabric suits 1-Click Clusters of 16 to 512 GPUs, and Lambda now quotes Superclusters from 4,000 GPUs.
