Section D · Position

Financial Shape

Lambda is private. Funding rounds and reported valuations are the main public anchors. The financial profile reflects a long-running company that scaled deliberately rather than venture-paced.

Funding history

Funding history through 2024-2025:

  • Early rounds 2014-2020 were comparatively small.
  • Series C in 2024 at ~$320M.
  • Series D in 2025 at $400M+.
  • Series E of more than $1.5B, announced 18 November 2025, led by TWG Global with USIT.
  • Strategic financing supplements the equity.

Valuation

Reported post-money valuations escalated through 2024-2025:

  • Series C at $1.5B+.
  • Series D at higher levels, into the $4-5B+ range based on reporting.
  • The Series E announcement states no valuation.

Revenue picture

Specific numbers aren't public. Reasonable inferences:

  • Annualized run rate in the high-hundreds-of-millions range by 2025-2026. This is a 2025 inference, and Lambda publishes no revenue figure.
  • Mostly cloud revenue, since the hardware business ended in August 2025.
  • Growing reserved-capacity book providing forward visibility.

Capital structure

Like other GPU clouds, Lambda funds GPU procurement through a blend of equity and debt. The debt component is meaningful but smaller in absolute terms than CoreWeave's. In 2026 Lambda closed a $1B senior secured credit facility on 7 May, which upsized a $275M facility from August 2025. It closed a $926M term loan B on 27 August, rated Baa2 by Moody's, for GPUs behind an unnamed investment-grade customer. It announced a $1B investment-grade fixed-rate delayed draw loan at 6.78% on 1 October, for three customer deployments. Each is secured on the GPUs and their contracted cash flows.

Trajectory

Lambda's financial trajectory through 2025-2026:

  • Revenue growing with capacity expansion.
  • Valuation escalating with each fundraise.
  • Path to IPO conceivable in coming years.
  • Continued reinvestment into capacity.

✓ Check yourself

Answer from memory before opening each one.

  1. Which parts of the financial picture are reported, and which are inferred?

    Show answer

    Equity rounds come from Lambda's announcements, and valuations come from reporting. Revenue is not public: the high-hundreds-of-millions run rate is inferred. See revenue picture.

  2. Why do GPU clouds pair equity with debt, and what does a reserved-capacity book add?

    Show answer

    GPUs are expensive assets bought ahead of revenue, and debt avoids diluting equity for every purchase. A growing reserved book gives forward visibility, which supports borrowing. Lambda's debt is smaller than CoreWeave's, though its three 2026 financings come to about $2.9B. See capital structure.

  3. What pressure does a rising valuation at each round create?

    Show answer

    Each round resets expectations, so revenue must keep growing fast enough to justify the next price. It also makes an IPO the natural exit, which the profile calls conceivable. See valuation and trajectory.

  4. If run-rate revenue is near $800 million and the valuation is $4.5 billion, what is the multiple, and how far do you trust it?

    Show answer

    About 5.6 times revenue. Treat it as an order of magnitude, because both inputs are unofficial estimates and the range could move it a lot. See valuation and revenue picture.

Takeaway

  • Lambda raised small rounds from 2014 to 2020, then a $320M Series C in 2024, a reported $400M or more Series D in 2025 and a $1.5B or more Series E in November 2025.
  • Reported valuations rose from $1.5B or more at Series C to roughly $4B to $5B or more at Series D.
  • Revenue is not public; the profile infers a high-hundreds-of-millions run rate, mostly from cloud.
  • GPU purchases are funded by equity and debt, with debt meaningful but smaller than CoreWeave's, including three secured financings in 2026 of $1B, $926M and $1B.
  • Revenue and valuation both keep growing, and an IPO is conceivable in coming years.
