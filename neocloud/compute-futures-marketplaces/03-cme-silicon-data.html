Section B · The exchanges

CME + Silicon Data

CME Group, the largest derivatives exchange operator globally, announced compute futures with Silicon Data in May 2026. This was the watershed moment for compute as a financialized commodity, but as of November 2026 the contracts are not yet trading.

CME's role

CME Group operates listed futures and options markets across asset classes — energy, agriculture, metals, financial. CME's involvement provides:

  • Established exchange infrastructure.
  • Regulatory relationships and frameworks.
  • Existing relationships with hedgers and speculators.
  • Clearing and settlement mechanisms.
  • Liquidity from CME's broader trading population.

Silicon Data's role

Silicon Data is a specialist provider focused on AI compute price data. The role:

  • Constructing and maintaining the reference price index.
  • Sourcing transaction data from neoclouds and other providers.
  • Publishing methodology and underlying data.
  • Operating the index in a manipulation-resistant manner.

Silicon Data is the analytical underpinning; CME is the trading venue.

May 2026 announcement

The CME-Silicon Data compute futures market was announced in May 2026. CME later set October 5, 2026 as the target listing date, subject to review by the CFTC. That date passed without a listing. On September 21, 2026 the CFTC extended its review of the NYMEX submission for the two contracts by 45 days, to the end of November 9, 2026, citing novel or complex issues. As of early November 2026 I found no CME announcement that trading has begun. Reported preparation before the announcement included the items below. I could not confirm them in the primary sources read for this refresh:

  • Methodology development through 2024-2025.
  • A regulatory submission, now under CFTC review.
  • Pilot programs with strategic counterparties.
  • Public consultation on contract specifications.

The announcement was the first from a major exchange on compute futures, a category-defining moment. It is an announcement and not yet a market.

Contract specifications

Initial contract specifications cover specific GPU types. The CFTC letter names the first pair as Silicon Data H100 Rental Index Futures and Silicon Data B200 Rental Index Futures, submitted by NYMEX. Silicon Data describes the contracts as cash settled and priced in dollars per GPU-hour. Monthly settlement follows standard commodity futures conventions.

Index methodology

The Silicon Data index aggregates transaction prices from observable sources, weighting by volume and recency. The exact methodology balances transparency with manipulation resistance.

Expected participants

Participants include:

  • Neoclouds hedging revenue.
  • AI customers locking forward compute costs.
  • Speculators taking views on AI compute trajectory.
  • Basis traders arbitraging spot-futures spreads.
  • Market makers providing liquidity.

✓ Check yourself

Answer from memory before opening each one.

  1. CME lists the contracts and Silicon Data builds the index. Why split those jobs, and what does each side bring?

    Show answer

    The exchange brings listing, clearing, settlement and regulatory standing; the index provider brings the transaction data and the methodology. Splitting them keeps the settlement price independent of the venue and lets each side be judged on its own job. See CME's role and Silicon Data's role.

  2. The index weights transactions by volume and recency. A trader with a large short position into expiry wants a lower index. How could they act, and what limits it?

    Show answer

    They could push low-priced transactions near expiry, when recent trades weigh most. Defenses include many data sources, volume weighting that discounts small trades, and a published methodology that can be audited. See index methodology.

  3. Which participant type adds liquidity without a view on price, and why do hedgers need them?

    Show answer

    Market makers quote both sides. Without them a hedger cannot enter or exit size without moving the price, especially in a young market. See expected participants.

  4. What separates an announced contract from a functioning market?

    Show answer

    Regulatory clearance, an actual listing, and then liquidity: tight spreads, depth in several months and diverse participants. CME later targeted October 5, 2026 for listing, subject to CFTC review. The CFTC then extended that review to November 9, 2026, so an announcement alone is not a market. See the May 2026 announcement.

Takeaway

  • CME supplies the venue, clearing and regulatory standing; Silicon Data supplies the index and the transaction data behind it.
  • The pair announced compute futures in May 2026 and later targeted October 5, 2026 for listing, subject to CFTC review. The CFTC extended its review to November 9, 2026, and no trading had begun as of early November 2026.
  • The first announced contracts reference H100 and B200 rental indices, with monthly settlement in the manner of other commodity futures.
  • The index weights observed transactions by volume and recency, which makes it useful but a target for expiry-day manipulation.
  • Expected users are neoclouds hedging revenue, AI buyers locking costs, speculators, basis traders and market makers.
