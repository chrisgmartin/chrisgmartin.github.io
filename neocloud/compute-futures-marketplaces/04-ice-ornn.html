Section B · The exchanges

ICE + Ornn

Intercontinental Exchange (ICE) announced compute futures with Ornn as the index provider on May 19, 2026, a week after CME's announcement. The first contracts set for listing reference the Ornn Compute Price Index (OCPI) for H100 and B200. No listing date is set as of November 2026.

ICE's role

ICE is one of the major derivatives exchange operators globally — a peer to CME with comparable infrastructure. ICE provides similar exchange functions: listing, clearing, settlement, regulatory framework.

Ornn's role

Ornn is the data and index provider partnered with ICE. The Ornn Compute Price Index (OCPI) is the reference for ICE-listed compute futures.

The OCPI index

The OCPI aggregates transaction prices for specific GPU types into a published index. The methodology differs in details from Silicon Data's but serves the same purpose.

The September 29, 2026 ICE notice names two first contracts: OCPI H100 Futures (symbol HPR) and OCPI B200 Futures (symbol BKL). Earlier editions of this chapter listed H200 and RTX 5090 as covered; the notice does not name them, and they may follow as the market evolves.

Contract lineup

ICE published specifications for both contracts in its September 2026 exchange notice:

  • Monthly futures, cash settled. Final settlement is the average of the daily OCPI prices for the contract month, built from hourly GPU prices.
  • Contract size is the number of hours in the contract month, so one contract covers one GPU for the month.
  • Prices are quoted in dollars per GPU-hour, with a minimum tick of $0.001.
  • Up to 24 consecutive monthly contracts, or as the exchange decides. Last trading day is the last business day of the contract month.

The notice says no listing date or timeline is set and that ICE continues to work with the CFTC. From October 5, 2026 ICE makes OCPI data, including hypothetical settlement prices, available to participants so they can prepare. That is data only, not trading.

vs CME / Silicon Data

The two exchanges compete for liquidity in the same underlying market. Whether liquidity coalesces on one exchange or persists across both is a meaningful market-structure question. Historical patterns in commodity futures suggest:

  • One exchange usually emerges as the primary venue.
  • Customer relationships and clearing efficiencies drive concentration.
  • The second venue serves niche needs or specific participant bases.

Which exchange wins compute futures isn't yet clear in November 2026, because neither is trading.

✓ Check yourself

Answer from memory before opening each one.

  1. A neocloud holds a short position on ICE and its customer hedges on CME. Why can the two hedges fail to offset, even on the same GPU type?

    Show answer

    The contracts settle to different indices with different methodologies, so the two prices can diverge. The leftover difference is basis risk. A perfect offset needs both legs on the same index. See the OCPI index and vs CME and Silicon Data.

  2. Why does liquidity in commodity futures tend to concentrate on one venue, and what does a hedger lose by choosing the smaller one?

    Show answer

    Liquidity attracts liquidity: customer relationships and clearing efficiency pull flow to the leader. On the smaller venue, spreads are wider and large orders move the price. See vs CME and Silicon Data.

  3. An H100-heavy neocloud must pick a venue. What criteria do you apply?

    Show answer

    Which index tracks the prices the neocloud actually realizes, where its customers and counterparties already clear, depth at the tenors it needs, and clearing and margin costs. Index fit comes first, because it sets the unhedged risk. See vs CME and Silicon Data.

  4. Why might a second venue survive even if one exchange becomes the primary one?

    Show answer

    It can serve niche needs or specific participant groups, for example participants already clearing with that exchange. Historical patterns in commodity futures show a primary venue with a smaller second one. See vs CME and Silicon Data.

Takeaway

  • ICE, paired with index provider Ornn, announced a competing compute futures offering a week after CME.
  • The OCPI aggregates transaction prices for H100, H200, B200, RTX 5090 and other references, and the first ICE contracts target H100 and B200.
  • ICE's H100 contract is monthly and cash settled, and is sized to the number of hours in the contract month.
  • Different indices make the two venues imperfect substitutes, so hedging across both leaves basis risk.
  • History suggests one venue becomes primary; which one is not yet clear.
