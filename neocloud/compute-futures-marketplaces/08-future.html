Section D · Future

Future

The longer-term implications of compute futures extend beyond simple trading. Industry capital structures, customer procurement patterns, and competitive dynamics could all evolve.

Scenarios

Scenario A: Compute futures become standard infrastructure

Liquidity grows; major industry participants routinely use the markets for hedging; capital structures evolve to use compute futures as collateral. Compute as a financial asset class is established.

Scenario B: Niche but persistent

Compute futures remain a niche market used by sophisticated participants. Liquidity stays moderate; broader industry impact is limited.

Scenario C: Market failure

Liquidity doesn't develop sufficiently; manipulation incidents undermine credibility; market is abandoned or restructured.

Industry impact

Even at modest liquidity, compute futures change the industry:

  • Pricing transparency improves with the published forward curve.
  • Capital allocation decisions get more sophisticated.
  • Customer-vendor negotiations have a market reference.
  • Risk management becomes more accessible to mid-market players.

New categories

Compute as a financial asset enables new business categories:

  • Specialized compute-hedging advisory.
  • Compute trading desks at financial firms.
  • Securitization structures backed by compute revenue.
  • Indices and ETFs tracking compute prices.

These will emerge gradually as the underlying markets mature.

What to watch

  • Trading volume growth at CME and ICE.
  • Open interest in different tenors.
  • Major hedging announcements from neoclouds.
  • Customer-side hedging adoption.
  • New index methodologies or competing exchanges.
  • Regulatory developments.

✓ Check yourself

Answer from memory before opening each one.

  1. Which observable would best tell you in twelve months whether the market is heading toward standard infrastructure or a niche?

    Show answer

    Open interest in deferred months together with hedging announcements from neoclouds. Volume alone can come from day traders; deferred open interest shows firms committing to hedges. See what to watch.

  2. A customer is negotiating a 12-month reserved contract. How does a published forward curve change the negotiation, even at modest liquidity?

    Show answer

    It gives both sides a market reference for the price of future capacity, so each can test the quote against the curve. See industry impact.

  3. What would push the market toward the failure scenario?

    Show answer

    Liquidity that never develops, or a manipulation incident that destroys trust in the index. Without either depth or credibility, hedgers leave. See scenarios.

  4. Which new category depends most on deep, liquid markets, and which could appear first?

    Show answer

    Securitization needs deep liquidity and trusted indices, so it comes last. Advisory and trading desks need less depth and arrive earlier. See new categories.

  5. Why track open interest by tenor and not only trading volume?

    Show answer

    Volume counts turnover, including short-term speculation. Open interest by tenor shows how much risk participants are holding, and in which months. See what to watch.

Takeaway

  • Three scenarios are plausible: standard infrastructure, a persistent niche, or market failure.
  • Even at modest liquidity, a published forward curve improves pricing transparency and gives negotiations a market reference.
  • New categories include compute hedging advisory, trading desks, securitizations and compute price indices and funds.
  • Open interest by tenor, hedging announcements, customer adoption, new indices and regulation are the indicators to watch.
  • The category-defining moment came in May 2026, but listing still awaited regulatory review in the autumn 2026 sources, and the impact is still ahead.
