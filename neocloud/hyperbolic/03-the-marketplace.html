Section B · The marketplace

The Marketplace

Hyperbolic's GPU product rents pooled supplier capacity at prices the company posts. The supply pool is smaller than Vast's; the curation is tighter, and since 2026 the buyer no longer picks a host.

Supply side

Hyperbolic's supply comes from independent GPU providers similar to the Vast / TensorDock model. The supply pool is meaningfully smaller — thousands rather than tens of thousands of GPUs — but more curated, with operational standards more like RunPod's Community Cloud than Vast's open marketplace. Suppliers negotiate terms with Hyperbolic's supply team, which says pricing depends on hardware, configuration, availability, commitment and deployment model, rather than listing at a price of their own.

Demand side

Demand-side customers include:

  • Indie ML developers.
  • Research customers.
  • Small AI companies running cost-sensitive workloads.
  • Hyperbolic's own inference workloads (the marketplace can supply inference compute too).

Pricing

In 2025 Hyperbolic's prices tracked the marketplace category, within 10 to 25% of Vast and TensorDock depending on GPU type and time. That comparison no longer fits. The company posts one starting price per GPU, refreshed weekly from the best supplier rates on its platform, so the buyer sees a reseller's list price rather than a spread of host bids.

As of November 2026 the company's GPU page shows starting prices per GPU-hour of $3.19 for H100 SXM, $3.99 for H200 and $5.99 for B200, refreshed weekly. It sells three tiers. On-demand has no commitment and bills hourly. Reserved clusters take commitments under one year, are paid upfront and run without preemption. Private cloud takes commitments over one year at the lowest rates and is priced by contract. One named customer, Higgsfield, moved to a reserved contract it says is 35% cheaper than on-demand.

vs Vast / TensorDock

  • Hyperbolic's marketplace is smaller. Liquidity at any given moment is thinner.
  • Curation and supply consistency are slightly better than Vast's average listing.
  • DX is competitive but doesn't lead the category.

Position in marketplace category

Hyperbolic's marketplace is a credible second-or-third-tier option in the broader marketplace category. Customers who shop around for the best price often check Hyperbolic alongside Vast / RunPod Community / TensorDock.

✓ Check yourself

Answer from memory before opening each one.

  1. How does Hyperbolic's supply pool differ from Vast's, and what does it trade away?

    Show answer

    It is smaller, in the thousands of GPUs rather than tens of thousands, and more curated, with standards closer to RunPod's Community Cloud than to Vast's open marketplace. It gives up size and liquidity for consistency. See supply side.

  2. Why does thin liquidity matter to someone renting GPUs?

    Show answer

    With fewer listings at any moment, the exact GPU type, count and region you want may not be available at the posted price. Price also moves by GPU type and over time. A bigger pool is more likely to have a match. See vs Vast / TensorDock.

  3. Hyperbolic posts $3.19 an hour for an H100 and a Vast host lists the same card at $2.70. What else do you weigh before choosing?

    Show answer

    Supply consistency, which the profile rates slightly better than Vast's average listing, and developer experience, which is competitive but not leading. Hyperbolic's figure is a posted rate for pooled, verified capacity, while the Vast figure is one host's bid, so you are paying for fewer unreliable hosts and a price that holds. If the job is short and restartable, the cheaper listing can still win. See pricing.

  4. Besides outside customers, who else draws on the marketplace, and why does it matter?

    Show answer

    Hyperbolic's own inference workloads can use marketplace compute. That is the link between the two products and the basis of the synergy claim. It also means some supply serves the company before it reaches renters. See demand side.

  5. A team needs eight H100s for three days of fine-tuning and checks four marketplaces. Where should Hyperbolic rank, and when does it win?

    Show answer

    The profile calls it a credible second- or third-tier option, so check it beside Vast, RunPod Community and TensorDock. It wins when its curated supply has the configuration available at a competitive price, or when the team expects to use the inference API afterwards. See position in the category.

Takeaway

  • Hyperbolic's marketplace draws on independent GPU providers and is smaller than Vast's, in the thousands of GPUs rather than tens of thousands.
  • Supply is more curated than Vast's open listings, closer to RunPod's Community Cloud in operating standards.
  • Hyperbolic posts its own prices and refreshes them weekly from supplier rates ($3.19 H100, $3.99 H200, $5.99 B200 in November 2026), so compare them with marketplace bids as a reseller's list, not as another bid.
  • Thinner liquidity is the practical cost of the smaller pool, because a specific configuration is less likely to be free.
  • The marketplace is competent but does not lead its category, and the pairing with inference is where Hyperbolic differentiates.
