Section C · Commercial

Pricing & Commercial

Nebius's commercial model centers on reserved capacity with on-demand as a secondary offering. Pricing for the major customer deals isn't public; on-demand pricing tracks the broader enterprise neocloud market.

Reserved capacity model

Like CoreWeave and Crusoe, Nebius's commercial activity centers on multi-year reserved capacity. The Microsoft deal is a flagship example; smaller customers also commit on shorter terms with discounts.

On-demand pricing

On-demand pricing for self-serve customers is competitive with the broader market. As read on the pricing page on 5 November 2026, on-demand prices per GPU-hour are $4.50 for H100, $5.40 for H200, $8.50 for B200 and $9.50 for B300. Preemptible spot starts at $0.79 for H100 and H200 and $0.99 for B200 and B300. GB200 NVL72 and reserved clusters are quoted by sales. The page shows no effective date, so check it before you quote it. In October 2026 Nebius also began publishing live spot prices and offering reserve auctions for fixed blocks of capacity.

Enterprise contracts

Enterprise contracts include standard MSA terms, custom pricing on volume, and dedicated account management. The Trust center lists SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001 and ISO 42001, among others.

vs CoreWeave

Nebius's pricing is comparable to CoreWeave's. The European-anchored option matters for some customers; pricing alone doesn't strongly differentiate.

✓ Check yourself

Answer from memory before opening each one.

  1. Where does Nebius's commercial activity centre, and what does that imply about how it finances capacity?

    Show answer

    On multi-year reserved capacity. Committed revenue supports the capital spent on GPUs and datacenters, with on-demand as a secondary offering. See reserved capacity model.

  2. Why is the pricing for the largest deals not public, while on-demand pricing is?

    Show answer

    The large deals are negotiated to the volume, term and delivery schedule. On-demand is a list price for self-serve buyers. See enterprise contracts and on-demand pricing.

  3. Nebius and CoreWeave quote about the same price per GPU hour. What does a buyer weigh instead?

    Show answer

    Location and data-residency needs, product breadth beyond raw compute, and which vendor can deliver the capacity on the date you need it. Price alone does not differentiate. See vs CoreWeave.

  4. A mid-size lab will run for six months and does not know its peak. Which commercial option fits, and what is the cost of choosing wrong?

    Show answer

    A shorter reservation sized to the base load, with on-demand for peaks. Too small a reservation sends the base load to the higher on-demand rate. Too large pays for idle GPUs. See reserved capacity model.

  5. What contract baseline does an enterprise buyer get, and which item would a regulated buyer test first?

    Show answer

    Standard MSA terms, volume pricing, SOC 2 compliance and a dedicated account manager. A regulated buyer should test the compliance attestations and data residency first. See enterprise contracts.

Takeaway

  • Nebius sells mostly multi-year reserved capacity, with on-demand as a secondary offering.
  • Pricing for the major deals is custom and not public.
  • On-demand prices are published and sit in the range of other top-tier neoclouds, so price rarely separates Nebius from peers.
  • Enterprise contracts carry an MSA, volume pricing, SOC 2 and account management.
  • The European option, not price, is what differentiates Nebius from CoreWeave for some buyers.
