Section C · Markets

Customer Segments

Different neoclouds win different customer segments. Mapping segment to vendor clarifies who competes for what.

Frontier labs

OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta AI, Google DeepMind, etc.

  • Primary providers: CoreWeave (Microsoft, Meta and OpenAI contracts), Crusoe (Stargate), Nebius (Microsoft and Meta). Lambda also serves Microsoft under a multibillion-dollar agreement signed in November 2025.
  • Why: Multi-thousand-GPU clusters; multi-billion-dollar commitments; deepest operational integration.
  • Off-limits: Marketplaces, smaller dedicated.

Mid-market AI

Series A through Series D AI startups and AI-focused mid-enterprises.

  • Primary providers: Lambda, RunPod Secure, sometimes Nebius for European customers.
  • Why: Smaller commitments; need reliability without negotiating CoreWeave-scale deals.
  • Marketplace use: Often for research / experimentation alongside production on dedicated.

Indie developers

Hobbyists, students, indie ML builders, ML consultancies.

  • Primary providers: Vast.AI, RunPod Community, TensorDock, Hyperbolic.
  • Why: Price-sensitive; high tolerance for variability; need broad hardware selection.
  • Off-limits: Enterprise neoclouds (don't sell at indie scale).

Enterprise IT

Non-AI-native enterprises adopting AI as one capability among many.

  • Primary providers: Hyperscalers, with CoreWeave / Nebius / Lambda as secondary options.
  • Why: Bundled with broader cloud relationships; enterprise procurement comfort.
  • Smaller neoclouds: Limited penetration here.

Inference-as-a-service

Companies wanting OpenAI-style per-token APIs.

  • Primary providers: Together.AI, Fireworks (not in this guide), Hyperbolic (inference side), RunPod Serverless.
  • Adjacent: Closed-source (OpenAI, Anthropic) for highest-quality use cases.

✓ Check yourself

Answer from memory before opening each one.

  1. A Series B startup with a 128-GPU training need asks whether to negotiate with CoreWeave. What does the segment map suggest?

    Show answer

    Lambda or RunPod Secure are the natural fit. CoreWeave-scale deals are built for frontier labs, and a mid-market buyer gets reliability without negotiating multi-billion-dollar terms. See mid-market AI.

  2. Why are the marketplaces off-limits to frontier labs, and the enterprise neoclouds off-limits to indie developers?

    Show answer

    Frontier labs need multi-thousand-GPU clusters with deep operational integration, which marketplace supply cannot provide. Enterprise neoclouds do not sell at indie scale, so each side's product shape rules out the other's buyer. See frontier labs.

  3. Why do mid-market teams often keep a marketplace account alongside a dedicated provider?

    Show answer

    They run research and experimentation cheaply on marketplace capacity and keep production on dedicated infrastructure with reliability and support. The two segments' needs coexist inside one team. See mid-market AI.

  4. A non-AI-native bank wants GPUs for a document pipeline. Why does the chapter point it at hyperscalers, and when would you move it to a neocloud?

    Show answer

    It already has a hyperscaler contract, procurement comfort and bundled services. Move it when GPU cost or availability under that contract becomes the constraint, or when it needs a cluster size the hyperscaler cannot supply on acceptable terms. See enterprise IT.

  5. What decides between Together.AI and a closed-source API for an inference product?

    Show answer

    Together serves open-source models at per-token prices, which suits teams that want control and cost. Closed-source APIs lead at the highest-quality use cases. The deciding factors are the quality you need and whether you must fine-tune or host open weights. See inference as a service.

Takeaway

  • Frontier labs buy multi-thousand-GPU clusters from CoreWeave, Crusoe and Nebius and are off-limits to marketplaces.
  • Mid-market AI companies choose Lambda or RunPod Secure for reliability at manageable commitments, often adding marketplace capacity for experiments.
  • Indie developers go to Vast, RunPod Community, TensorDock and Hyperbolic because they are price-sensitive and tolerate variability.
  • Enterprise IT mostly stays with hyperscalers because of bundled relationships and procurement comfort.
  • Inference buyers choose among Together, Fireworks, Hyperbolic and RunPod Serverless, with closed APIs as the alternative.
  • No single neocloud wins every segment, because the market is heterogeneous by design.
