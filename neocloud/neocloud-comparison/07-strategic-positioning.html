Section C · Markets

Strategic Positioning

Where each company has structural advantage and where they're vulnerable.

Structural advantages by company

  • CoreWeave: Scale, NVIDIA relationship, customer commitments, operational maturity.
  • Crusoe: Energy cost basis, Stargate involvement.
  • Nebius: European positioning, engineering depth, public capital access, and a Platinum rating in ClusterMAX 3.0.
  • Lambda: Brand and customer relationships from hardware heritage, mid-market segment fit.
  • Together.AI: Research credibility, optimization stack, lifecycle product.
  • RunPod: DX investment, two-product flexibility, Serverless offering.
  • Hyperbolic: Founder credibility, dual-product story.
  • Vast.AI: Supply density, marketplace network effects, capital efficiency.
  • TensorDock: Supply curation, customer consistency.

Vulnerabilities by company

  • CoreWeave: Customer concentration (Microsoft was 67% of 2025 revenue; the top customer fell to 36% in the second quarter of 2026), NVIDIA dependency, debt-financed cap-ex risk.
  • Crusoe: Stargate concentration, energy advantage finite, ESG narrative, and a fall to Bronze in ClusterMAX 3.0.
  • Nebius: Geopolitical heritage, customer concentration, public-market pressure.
  • Lambda: Scale ceiling, reliance on debt for buildout ($1B-scale financings in May, August and October 2026), DX gap, and a change of chief executive in May 2026.
  • Together.AI: Hyperscaler absorption risk, margin compression, and a reliability downgrade to Bronze in ClusterMAX 3.0.
  • RunPod: Sub-scale on both axes vs specialists.
  • Hyperbolic: Sub-scale on both axes, brand recognition gap.
  • Vast.AI: Segment ceiling at enterprise; can't serve compliance-bound customers.
  • TensorDock: Scale vs Vast, brand recognition.

Moat strength (subjective ranking)

From strongest to weakest moat:

  1. CoreWeave (scale + customer commitments + NVIDIA).
  2. Crusoe (energy + Stargate inner-circle).
  3. Nebius (engineering + European positioning + public-market capital).
  4. Vast.AI (supply density + marketplace network).
  5. Lambda (brand + segment fit).
  6. Together.AI (research + optimization stack).
  7. RunPod (DX + product breadth).
  8. Hyperbolic (founder credibility + dual product).
  9. TensorDock (curation vs competitors).

✓ Check yourself

Answer from memory before opening each one.

  1. CoreWeave's strongest advantage and a top vulnerability both come from the same fact. Name it and explain.

    Show answer

    Large customer commitments from a few buyers. They give scale and contracted revenue, which is the moat, and they create concentration risk, which is the vulnerability if a buyer shifts to its own capacity or renegotiates. See vulnerabilities by company.

  2. Why is Crusoe's energy cost advantage a moat that the chapter also lists as a vulnerability?

    Show answer

    Cheap stranded power lowers its cost basis. But the supply of such sites is finite, so the advantage does not scale without limit, and the ESG narrative around gas-powered compute adds tension. See vulnerabilities by company.

  3. The moat ranking puts Vast.AI above Lambda. What is the reasoning, and why might you disagree?

    Show answer

    Vast has supply density, network effects and capital efficiency, while Lambda leans on brand and segment fit. You might disagree if you weight enterprise relationships and cluster products over marketplace liquidity. The ranking is subjective and the chapter says so. See moat strength.

  4. Together.AI's vulnerability is hyperscaler absorption. Describe the sequence by which that would happen.

    Show answer

    A hyperscaler sells the same open models per token, bundled with its cloud, at a price that compresses Together's margin. Customers with existing contracts move to the bundle, so the independent platform loses volume or price. See vulnerabilities by company.

  5. RunPod and Hyperbolic are both described as sub-scale on both axes. What does that mean for their strategy?

    Show answer

    Each runs two product lines against specialists in both, so neither wins on scale in either. The bet is that cross-selling and developer experience offset the scale gap. It works only if customer lifetime value across both products outweighs the duplicated cost. See vulnerabilities by company.

Takeaway

  • CoreWeave's moat combines scale, NVIDIA relationships and commitments, and its exposure is customer concentration, NVIDIA dependency and debt-financed capital spending.
  • Crusoe's cost basis and Stargate role are real advantages, but concentration and finite cheap-power sites limit them.
  • Nebius pairs European positioning and engineering depth with geopolitical heritage and customer concentration.
  • Together.AI's research credibility is exposed to hyperscaler absorption and margin compression.
  • RunPod, Hyperbolic and TensorDock are coherent in their segment but sub-scale against specialists or Vast.
  • The moat ranking is a subjective judgement and should be argued with, not quoted.
