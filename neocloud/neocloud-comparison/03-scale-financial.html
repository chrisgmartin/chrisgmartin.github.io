Section B · Dimensions

Scale & Financial

Revenue, funding, valuation, and customer commitments across the cast. The gaps between the leaders and the smaller players are enormous.

Revenue scale (approximate annualized run-rate, 2025-2026)

  • CoreWeave: Multi-billion (public disclosure).
  • Crusoe: High hundreds of millions to low billions (estimated).
  • Nebius: Hundreds of millions, scaling rapidly (public disclosure).
  • Lambda: Hundreds of millions (estimated).
  • Together.AI: Nine-figure annualized (estimated).
  • RunPod: High-eight-figure to low-nine-figure (estimated).
  • Vast.AI: Eight-figure (estimated).
  • Hyperbolic: Mid-eight-figure (estimated).
  • TensorDock: Mid-seven to eight-figure (estimated).

The gap from CoreWeave at the top to TensorDock at the bottom is two-plus orders of magnitude.

Funding raised

  • CoreWeave: Multi-billion equity + multi-billion debt; now public.
  • Crusoe: $1B+ equity + significant debt and project finance.
  • Nebius: Public; large capital base from relisting.
  • Lambda: Several hundred million across rounds.
  • Together.AI: Several hundred million.
  • RunPod: Series A scale.
  • Hyperbolic: Series A scale.
  • Vast.AI: Minimal disclosed funding.
  • TensorDock: Seed / Series A scale.

Valuation

  • Public companies (CoreWeave, Nebius) have market-cap-determined valuations in the tens of billions.
  • Private companies' latest reported valuations range from billions (Crusoe, Together, Lambda) down to high hundreds of millions for smaller players.

Customer commitments

  • CoreWeave: $35B+ across Microsoft and Meta over multi-year terms.
  • Crusoe: Stargate-related capacity in the multi-tens to hundreds of billions exposure.
  • Nebius: $17B+ Microsoft, plus Meta and others.
  • Lambda, Together, RunPod: Many smaller commitments aggregating to substantial books but no individual marquee scale.
  • Marketplaces: No reserved commitments — pay-as-you-go.

Capital structure

  • Debt-heavy: CoreWeave, Crusoe, Nebius.
  • Equity-heavy: Together, Hyperbolic.
  • Lightly capitalized: Vast, TensorDock.

✓ Check yourself

Answer from memory before opening each one.

  1. A deck claims Crusoe has Stargate exposure in the hundreds of billions. Which figures in this chapter are public disclosure and which are estimates, and how does that change how you use them?

    Show answer

    CoreWeave and Nebius figures come from public disclosure, so they can be checked in filings. Revenue for the private companies is estimated, and the Stargate exposure is a range, not a contracted number. Cite the first group as fact and the second as an estimate with its source. See revenue scale.

  2. Why can CoreWeave and Vast.AI both be valid businesses at a scale gap of more than two orders of magnitude?

    Show answer

    They sell different things to different buyers. CoreWeave funds multi-billion capital spending against multi-year commitments, while Vast takes a fee on supply others own and needs little capital. Each strategy fits its own segment. See capital structure.

  3. CoreWeave's commitments are $35B+ and Nebius's are $17B+, yet neither number is revenue. What does a commitment tell you, and what does it not?

    Show answer

    It tells you demand is contracted over multi-year terms, which supports the debt that funds the build. It does not tell you when revenue arrives, or that the customer cannot renegotiate. Read it as a backlog with delivery and counterparty risk. See customer commitments.

  4. Pick a debt-heavy and an equity-heavy company from the chapter. What breaks first in each if utilization falls?

    Show answer

    The debt-heavy builder, such as CoreWeave, faces fixed interest and covenants against GPU-backed facilities, so a revenue shortfall stresses the balance sheet quickly. The equity-heavy platform, such as Together, has no debt service, so it burns cash and can slow spending or raise again instead. See capital structure.

  5. Public valuations are in the tens of billions and private ones range from billions to high hundreds of millions. What makes the two sets hard to compare?

    Show answer

    Market cap moves daily with sentiment, while private valuations are set at funding rounds and reflect that round's terms. Compare on revenue, backlog and growth, and treat the two valuation sets as different kinds of number. See valuation.

Takeaway

  • Revenue runs from multi-billion at CoreWeave to a few million at the smallest marketplaces, a gap of more than two orders of magnitude.
  • Only CoreWeave and Nebius report figures publicly; the rest of the revenue and valuation numbers are estimates.
  • Customer commitments such as $35B+ at CoreWeave and $17B+ at Nebius are contracted backlog, not revenue.
  • CoreWeave, Crusoe and Nebius are debt-heavy, Together and Hyperbolic equity-heavy, and Vast and TensorDock lightly capitalized.
  • Marketplaces carry no reserved commitments, so their revenue is pay-as-you-go and scales with usage.
