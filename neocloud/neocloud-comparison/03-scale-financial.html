Section B · Dimensions

Scale & Financial

Revenue, funding, valuation, and customer commitments across the cast. The gaps between the leaders and the smaller players are enormous.

Revenue scale (as of November 2026)

  • CoreWeave: $5.1B of revenue in 2025 and $4.65B in the first half of 2026 (filings).
  • Crusoe: No revenue filed. It reported Managed Inference above $100M in annual recurring revenue in September 2026 (company statement); total revenue is an estimate in the high hundreds of millions to low billions.
  • Nebius: $582.3M of group revenue in the second quarter of 2026 and AI cloud annual run-rate revenue of $3.0B (filings).
  • Lambda: Hundreds of millions (estimated). Lambda has not published revenue.
  • Together.AI: Nine-figure annualized (estimated).
  • RunPod: Over $325M in annual recurring revenue in October 2026, up from $120M in January 2026 (company statement).
  • Vast.AI: Eight-figure (estimated).
  • Hyperbolic: About $15M in annual recurring revenue at the end of 2025, with a $150M target for the end of 2026 (company statement).
  • TensorDock: Mid-seven to eight-figure (estimated).

The gap from CoreWeave at the top to TensorDock at the bottom is two-plus orders of magnitude.

Funding raised

  • CoreWeave: Multi-billion equity + multi-billion debt; public since March 2025. NVIDIA invested a further $2B in January 2026.
  • Crusoe: $3.9B Series F at a $30.9B post-money valuation, announced as an initial closing in September 2026, plus significant debt and project finance.
  • Nebius: Public; large capital base from relisting. It signed a $3.5B bridge loan in October 2026 for GPU buildout.
  • Lambda: Over $1.5B in equity, including a Series E of over $1.5B in November 2025, plus $1B-scale debt facilities in May, August and October 2026.
  • Together.AI: $800M Series C in July 2026, after a $305M Series B. It also reports over 500 MW of compute commitments that its investors will fund separately.
  • RunPod: $100M led by Summit Partners in June 2026 at a $1.0B valuation.
  • Hyperbolic: $30M Series A1 led by Walden Catalyst in October 2026.
  • Vast.AI: Minimal disclosed funding.
  • TensorDock: Seed / Series A scale.

Valuation

  • Public companies (CoreWeave, Nebius) have market-cap-determined valuations that change every day; read them off an exchange quote.
  • Private companies' latest stated valuations are $30.9B for Crusoe (September 2026) and $1.0B for RunPod (June 2026). Together.AI and Lambda have not stated a current valuation.

Customer commitments

  • CoreWeave: $103.7B of unsatisfied remaining performance obligations at 30 June 2026. Meta order forms of up to $14.2B (September 2025) and $21.0B (March 2026), and OpenAI up to $11.9B (March 2025) and $6.5B (September 2025). Microsoft was 67% of 2025 revenue.
  • Crusoe: Over $140B of total contracted value and over 6 GW of contracted capacity (company statement, September 2026).
  • Nebius: About $17.4B from Microsoft through 2031, rising to about $19.4B if Microsoft buys more. Meta signed about $3B in November 2025 and up to $27B in March 2026 ($12B dedicated capacity and up to $15B for capacity Meta buys if no one else does).
  • Lambda: A multibillion-dollar, multi-year Microsoft agreement for tens of thousands of NVIDIA GPUs (November 2025); Lambda gives no dollar figure.
  • Together, RunPod: Many smaller commitments aggregating to substantial books but no individual marquee scale.
  • Marketplaces: No reserved commitments — pay-as-you-go.

Capital structure

  • Debt-heavy: CoreWeave, Crusoe, Nebius, and increasingly Lambda.
  • Equity-heavy: Together, Hyperbolic.
  • Lightly capitalized: Vast, TensorDock.

✓ Check yourself

Answer from memory before opening each one.

  1. A deck claims Crusoe has contracted value above $140 billion. Which figures in this chapter come from filings and which are company statements or estimates, and how does that change how you use them?

    Show answer

    CoreWeave and Nebius figures come from filings, so they can be checked there. Crusoe, RunPod and Hyperbolic figures are company statements that no auditor has reviewed, and the remaining private revenue figures are estimates. Cite the first group as fact and the others with their source and date. See revenue scale.

  2. Why can CoreWeave and Vast.AI both be valid businesses at a scale gap of more than two orders of magnitude?

    Show answer

    They sell different things to different buyers. CoreWeave funds multi-billion capital spending against multi-year commitments, while Vast takes a fee on supply others own and needs little capital. Each strategy fits its own segment. See capital structure.

  3. CoreWeave reports $103.7B of remaining performance obligations and Nebius a Microsoft contract of about $17.4B, yet neither number is revenue. What does a commitment tell you, and what does it not?

    Show answer

    It tells you demand is contracted over multi-year terms, which supports the debt that funds the build. It does not tell you when revenue arrives, or that the customer cannot renegotiate. Read it as a backlog with delivery and counterparty risk. See customer commitments.

  4. Pick a debt-heavy and an equity-heavy company from the chapter. What breaks first in each if utilization falls?

    Show answer

    The debt-heavy builder, such as CoreWeave, faces fixed interest and covenants against GPU-backed facilities, so a revenue shortfall stresses the balance sheet quickly. The equity-heavy platform, such as Together, has no debt service, so it burns cash and can slow spending or raise again instead. See capital structure.

  5. Public valuations are market caps and private ones are round prices such as $30.9B for Crusoe and $1.0B for RunPod. What makes the two sets hard to compare?

    Show answer

    Market cap moves daily with sentiment, while private valuations are set at funding rounds and reflect that round's terms. Compare on revenue, backlog and growth, and treat the two valuation sets as different kinds of number. See valuation.

Takeaway

  • Revenue runs from multi-billion at CoreWeave to a few million at the smallest marketplaces, a gap of more than two orders of magnitude.
  • Only CoreWeave and Nebius file audited figures; the rest are company statements or estimates.
  • Customer commitments such as $103.7B of obligations at CoreWeave and about $17.4B from Microsoft at Nebius are contracted backlog, not revenue.
  • CoreWeave, Crusoe and Nebius are debt-heavy, Together and Hyperbolic equity-heavy, and Vast and TensorDock lightly capitalized.
  • Marketplaces carry no reserved commitments, so their revenue is pay-as-you-go and scales with usage.
