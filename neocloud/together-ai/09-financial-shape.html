Section D · Position

Financial Shape

Together is private; financial detail is limited. Funding history and reported valuations are the main public anchors. The economic profile differs from capital-intensive GPU clouds — software margins matter more, GPU cap-ex less.

Funding history

Public funding rounds, approximate amounts:

  • Seed in 2022.
  • Series A in 2023.
  • Series B in 2024 (~$100M+).
  • Subsequent rounds in 2024-2025 supporting infrastructure expansion.

Total equity capital raised is in the several-hundred-million range. Investors include strategic VCs and infrastructure-focused funds.

Valuation

  • Series B valuation reportedly in the low billions.
  • Subsequent rounds at higher valuations.
  • Together is now a unicorn-tier company by valuation.

Revenue picture

Specific revenue numbers aren't public. Reasonable inferences:

  • Annualized run-rate in the meaningful nine-figure range by 2025-2026.
  • Heavy growth on per-token API traffic.
  • Meaningful share from enterprise dedicated commitments.
  • Training and fine-tuning revenue secondary but real.

Unit economics

The per-token unit economics depend on:

  • Inference cost (GPU time, electricity).
  • Serving optimization that determines tokens-per-second per GPU.
  • Effective utilization across the GPU fleet.
  • Customer-paid revenue per token.

Together's research-driven optimization compresses cost-per-token below less-optimized competitors. Margins on per-token revenue are reasonable; gross margins compress as competitors converge on similar optimizations.

Growth trajectory

Together's growth rate through 2024-2026 has been strong but not the order-of-magnitude pace CoreWeave saw. The per-token model scales linearly with customer adoption rather than mechanically with infrastructure deployment. Customer acquisition and traffic growth are the constraints.

Path to profitability

The path to sustainable profitability requires:

  • Sufficient gross margin on per-token revenue.
  • Scaling operating costs (R&D, sales, support) sub-linearly with revenue.
  • Continued growth in customer base and traffic.

The model is more software-like than capital-intensive-GPU-cloud-like. Profitability should follow as scale builds, provided per-token margins hold.

✓ Check yourself

Answer from memory before opening each one.

  1. Why does a per-token business grow with customer adoption, while a GPU rental business grows with deployed infrastructure?

    Show answer

    Rental revenue follows the GPUs sold under contract. Per-token revenue appears only when customers send traffic, so installed capacity can sit idle. The growth limit is acquiring customers and traffic. See growth trajectory.

  2. Name the terms that set gross margin per token, and say which one the research team moves most.

    Show answer

    Inference cost (GPU time and power), tokens per second per GPU, fleet utilisation and revenue per token. Research moves tokens per second per GPU, which lowers cost per token. See unit economics.

  3. A $2.50 GPU serves 3,000 tokens per second when busy. What is the cost per million tokens at 40% utilisation, and at 80%?

    Show answer

    At 40% it serves 4.32M tokens an hour, so a million cost about $0.58. At 80% it serves 8.64M, so a million cost about $0.29. Utilisation halves cost as readily as a software gain does. See unit economics.

  4. Margins compress as competitors converge on the same optimizations. Why does that happen, and what protects margin?

    Show answer

    Published techniques spread, rivals reach similar throughput, and competition passes the saving to customers. Protection comes from the next optimization, better utilisation and higher-margin products such as fine-tuning and dedicated deployments. See unit economics.

  5. What would make you doubt that Together is software-like rather than infrastructure-like?

    Show answer

    Gross margin staying low because GPUs dominate cost, or prices falling faster than cost per token. Then it would look like a capital-heavy infrastructure business. See path to profitability.

Takeaway

  • Together is private, so funding rounds and reported valuations are the main public anchors and revenue figures are estimates.
  • Its economics are those of a software platform: gross margin per token matters more than GPU capital spending.
  • Cost per token depends on tokens per second per GPU, fleet utilisation and revenue per token, and research-driven optimization lowers it.
  • Growth follows customer adoption and traffic rather than infrastructure deployed, so it runs slower than CoreWeave's.
  • Profit needs per-token gross margin to hold while operating costs grow slower than revenue.
