Section D · Position

Customers

Together's customer base spans AI startups, enterprises adding AI features, and platform companies building AI-powered products. The per-token-API surface lowers the barrier to adoption.

Customer segments

  • AI startups building AI-native products. They want cheap inference and the option to fine-tune or self-host as they scale.
  • SaaS companies adding AI features. They want OpenAI-compatible APIs they can swap into without rewriting their integration.
  • Enterprises with AI initiatives. They want open-source alternatives to closed-source frontier APIs for cost, customization, or data-control reasons.
  • AI tooling platforms. Companies whose product is a layer over LLM APIs often use Together as one of their backends.
  • Research customers. Academic or quasi-academic users running experiments on hosted models.
  • Named customers. The Series C post (July 2026) names Cognition, Decagon, Eleven Labs, Cursor and Suno. The Series B post (February 2025) named Salesforce, Zoom, SK Telecom, Hedra, Cognition, Zomato, Krea, Cartesia and The Washington Post. Both lists are Together's own choice of references.

Why customers pick Together

  • Per-token cost meaningfully below closed-source frontier APIs.
  • OpenAI-API compatibility — minimal integration work to switch.
  • Broad model catalog including the latest open-source releases quickly after their public availability.
  • Lifecycle support — same platform for inference, fine-tuning, and training.
  • Research-credibility-driven confidence in serving quality.
  • Open posture on the underlying stack and models.

Who Together wins

  • Cost-conscious AI builders looking for OpenAI alternatives.
  • Customers fine-tuning models who want a managed fine-tuning experience.
  • Customers who want one platform across the model lifecycle.
  • Customers who value the research-credibility brand.

Who Together loses

  • Customers who need absolute frontier capability — closed-source still wins on hardest tasks.
  • Customers already deep into a hyperscaler ecosystem (AWS Bedrock, Azure AI, Vertex AI) who'd rather consolidate.
  • Customers running production inference at scale enough to justify their own infrastructure (they'd be on CoreWeave or similar).
  • Customers who need full control over the inference stack (they'd self-host on raw GPU).

Workload patterns

What customers actually run:

  • Chatbots and conversational AI front-ends.
  • RAG (retrieval-augmented generation) workflows.
  • Document summarization and extraction.
  • Code generation and assistance.
  • Customer-support automation.
  • Content generation (marketing, internal documents, etc.).
  • Embedding generation for search and recommendation.
  • Agentic workflows that string together multiple LLM calls.

The mix is broadly representative of the production-AI workload distribution across the industry.

Geographic distribution

Together's customer base is global. The cloud-API model travels well — customers anywhere with network access can use the platform. US and European customers dominate; Asia-Pacific is growing.

✓ Check yourself

Answer from memory before opening each one.

  1. How does OpenAI compatibility change the cost of winning a customer, and what does it do to retention?

    Show answer

    It lets a customer trial Together in hours, which lowers acquisition cost. It lowers the cost of leaving by the same amount, so Together must keep winning on price, quality and reliability, with lock-in coming mainly from fine-tuning and the wider lifecycle. See why customers pick Together.

  2. A company that spends heavily on inference decides to build its own cluster. Where did Together's value stop?

    Show answer

    Past a certain scale, running your own infrastructure costs less than paying a per-token margin. The customer then moves to a GPU cloud. The per-token price is the cost of not operating the fleet yourself. See who Together loses.

  3. A bank already on Azure wants open-model summarisation. What pushes it toward the hyperscaler, and what toward Together?

    Show answer

    Procurement, security review and consolidated billing push it to Azure. Lower price, faster access to new models and serving speed push it to Together. The decision turns on whether the model it wants is available there at a tolerable price. See who Together loses.

  4. An agent makes ten sequential model calls per task. How does that change what the customer cares about compared with a single chat call?

    Show answer

    Latency and failures compound across calls: ten two-second calls take twenty seconds, and one failure can break the chain. Speed, reliability and per-token price all matter more. See workload patterns.

  5. The chapter says Together's customers are more SaaS and startup than CoreWeave's. What does that imply for revenue shape?

    Show answer

    Revenue comes from many accounts billed by usage, so concentration risk is lower but commitment is also lower and spend can move with traffic. See customer segments.

Takeaway

  • Customers are AI startups, SaaS firms adding AI features, enterprises seeking open alternatives, tooling platforms and researchers.
  • They choose Together for lower per-token cost, OpenAI-API compatibility, a broad catalog that tracks new releases, lifecycle support and confidence in the serving stack.
  • Together wins cost-conscious builders, customers who fine-tune and teams that want one platform across the model lifecycle.
  • It loses customers who need frontier capability, are deep in a hyperscaler, run inference at a scale that justifies their own infrastructure, or need full control of the stack.
  • Typical workloads are chatbots, retrieval-augmented generation, extraction, code assistance, support automation, embeddings and agentic chains.
