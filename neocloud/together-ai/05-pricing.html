Section C · Commercial

Pricing & Commercial

Together's pricing follows the per-token model for inference and dedicated-rental for higher-throughput / training workloads. The per-token rates undercut the top closed tiers, match the cheapest closed tier and are identical to direct peers for the same model.

Dedicated endpoint pricing

For customers whose workload exceeds the economical threshold of per-token pricing, dedicated inference bills per GPU for the time it runs (the docs say per GPU-minute), and provisioned throughput sells reserved capacity in throughput units with an SLA. The break-even between per-token and dedicated depends on traffic patterns; high-utilization customers benefit from dedicated.

The pricing page lists on-demand dedicated rates per GPU-hour as of November 2026: $5.49 for an HGX H100 and $8.99 for an HGX B200. H200, B300 and the NVL72 racks show "contact us", and reserved rates are by quote.

Fine-tuning pricing

The pricing page (November 2026) prices fine-tuning per million training tokens, with a minimum charge per job. It lists LoRA and full fine-tuning on separate tabs, and supervised and preference training (DPO) at different rates. The supervised rates it shows by default include:

  • Small models (Qwen3.5 9B, Llama 3.1 8B): $0.34 per million tokens, $4 minimum.
  • Mid-size (Llama 3.3 70B): $2.03 per million tokens, $4 minimum.
  • Large (Qwen3.5 397B A17B): $5.60 per million tokens, $22 minimum. Kimi K2.6 is $15.
  • Total cost is the rate times your dataset tokens times epochs, so a full fine-tune of a large model can still reach thousands of dollars.

The resulting fine-tuned model can be served via the platform at standard per-token rates, or as a dedicated endpoint.

Training cluster pricing

GPU clusters list on-demand rates per GPU-hour as of November 2026: HGX H100 $3.99, H200 $5.99, B200 $8.19 and B300 $9.99. Preemptible rates start at $1.99 for an H100. Reserved terms from 3 to 180 days step the H100 down from $3.69 to $3.19, and 181+ days are by quote. A separate post-training service bills per minute for each active resource, with a promotional HGX B200 rate valid to 14 December 2026.

Together's positioning: bundling training with the inference and fine-tuning lifecycle. Customers who'll deploy their trained model on Together's inference API get strategic continuity that pure GPU clouds don't offer.

vs OpenAI / Anthropic

The gap to closed frontier APIs now depends on which two models you compare. Per million tokens, as of November 2026:

  • Claude Fable 5.1: $10 input, $50 output. Opus 5.5: $4 and $20. Sonnet 5.5: $2 and $10. Haiku 5.5: $0.10 and $0.50 (Anthropic pricing page).
  • Together GLM-5.3: $1.40 input, $4.40 output. That is 30% and 56% below Sonnet 5.5, and 65% and 78% below Opus 5.5.
  • Together Kimi K3 ($3 and $15) costs more than Sonnet 5.5. Together GLM-5.3-Flash ($0.15 and $0.50) is no cheaper than Haiku 5.5 on output.

The savings are real against the top closed tiers and small or gone against the cheap ones. Together's own July 2026 Series C post claims 6x to 20x lower costs for companies on open models, and cites Decagon cutting inference costs sixfold. Treat that as a company claim and test your own pair.

The quality gap matters case-by-case. For reasoning-heavy tasks, closed-source frontier still leads. For simpler chat, RAG, structured-output, and many enterprise tasks, open-source quality is sufficient.

vs Fireworks / Anyscale

Among the direct managed-inference competitors, pricing is now nearly identical for the same model. Fireworks lists Kimi K3 at $3.00 input and $15.00 output, GLM 5.3 at $1.40 and $4.40, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash at $0.30 and $1.20, and MiniMax M3 at $0.30 and $1.20. These are the same figures Together lists (both pages, November 2026). The competition has compressed margins across the category. Customers choose based on:

  • Specific model availability and quality.
  • Serving latency.
  • Reliability.
  • Customer support and platform polish.
  • Lifecycle product breadth (fine-tuning, training, etc.).

Unit economics

Together's per-token revenue translates to:

  • Cost of inference per token (GPU time, electricity, platform overhead).
  • Gross margin on the inference revenue.
  • Platform operating costs that the gross margin covers.

Together's research-driven optimization (FlashAttention, speculative decoding, etc.) compresses the cost-per-token, giving Together more margin headroom at competitive prices. This is a real unit-economic advantage.

✓ Check yourself

Answer from memory before opening each one.

  1. Suppose a model costs $0.80 per million tokens on the per-token API, or $4 an hour on a dedicated deployment that sustains 2,000 tokens per second. At what monthly volume does dedicated break even, and what utilisation is that?

    Show answer

    A month is about 730 hours, so dedicated costs about $2,920. At $0.80 per million that buys 3.65B tokens. Full-load capacity is 2,000 × 3,600 × 730, about 5.26B tokens, so break-even is near 70% utilisation. See dedicated endpoint pricing.

  2. An open model costs 30 to 56% less per token than a mid-tier closed model, and nothing less against the cheapest tier. Why is that not the whole decision?

    Show answer

    Quality on your task decides how many tokens, retries and corrections you need, and closed models still lead on some hard tasks. Compare cost per correct answer, pair by pair, and include migration and evaluation effort. See vs OpenAI / Anthropic.

  3. Peers list the same price for the same model. What do customers compare instead, and what does it say about pricing power?

    Show answer

    They compare model availability, latency, reliability, support and lifecycle breadth. Pricing power is limited, so differentiation has to come from those factors. See vs Fireworks / Anyscale.

  4. A competitor cuts the price of a model you also serve by 30%. What decides whether you follow?

    Show answer

    Your cost per token. If your tokens per GPU are higher, you can match the cut and keep margin. If not, matching erodes margin and holding price risks losing customers. See unit economics.

Takeaway

  • Inference is priced per million tokens with a published price per model, and Together tends to track the market rather than lead on price.
  • Dedicated inference prices reserved hardware per GPU-minute and provisioned throughput prices reserved capacity in throughput units. The break-even against per-token billing depends on traffic and utilisation.
  • Fine-tuning is priced per million training tokens with a minimum charge, from $0.34 for small models to $5.60 or more for large ones (default supervised rates).
  • Training clusters list at $3.99 to $9.99 per GPU-hour on demand, with reserved discounts for longer commitments.
  • Open models on Together are 30 to 90% cheaper than the top closed tiers, no cheaper than the smallest closed tier, and priced the same as direct peers for the same model.
  • Research-driven optimization lowers cost per token and leaves more margin at the same price.
