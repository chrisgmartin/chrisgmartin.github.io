Section C · Commercial

Infrastructure

Together blends owned hardware with strategic partner capacity. The hybrid model keeps capital efficiency higher than pure-owned competitors while supporting the operational quality enterprise customers expect.

The hybrid infrastructure model

Together's infrastructure is a mix of:

  • Owned GPU hardware in partner datacenters.
  • Capacity rented or reserved from major neocloud partners.
  • Strategic partnerships for specific deployments.

The blended approach lets Together scale faster than pure-owned models would allow while maintaining operational control over the customer-facing platform.

Owned hardware

For high-utilization inference workloads, owning the GPUs delivers better unit economics than renting. Together has built up substantial owned capacity over 2023-2026 for this purpose. The owned fleet:

  • Concentrated on current-generation datacenter cards (H100/H200/Blackwell).
  • Located in partner datacenters with appropriate networking.
  • Operated under Together's software stack.

The owned-hardware investment is one of the larger capital uses for the funding Together has raised. The July 2026 Series C post adds that over 500 MW of compute capacity will be financed independently by the new investors, so part of the growth sits on other balance sheets.

Partner capacity

For workloads that exceed owned capacity, Together leverages partnerships with the broader neocloud ecosystem. The model is opaque to customers — they see "Together inference" regardless of which underlying capacity serves a request.

A concrete case is the IBM Cloud deal announced in October 2026: a dedicated NVIDIA B300 cluster for inference, where Together runs the inference layer, IBM provides the cloud and NVIDIA supplies the GPUs and networking. Together says it is the first customer on that cluster.

This partnership flexibility is similar to RunPod Serverless's approach. The platform abstracts the underlying capacity question.

GPU mix

Together's GPU footprint spans:

  • H100 (the workhorse during the 2023-2024 ramp).
  • H200 for larger-memory inference and fine-tuning.
  • B200 and B300 (Blackwell) for newer deployments, and GB200 and GB300 NVL72 racks, all named on the pricing page as of November 2026. The site announced on-demand B200s on GPU clusters.
  • Some A100 legacy capacity.

Consumer cards aren't in the mix. The inference platform's economics work better on datacenter-grade hardware where serving optimization can extract maximum throughput.

Network

Inference workloads have different network needs from training:

  • Lower inter-GPU bandwidth requirements for many inference patterns.
  • Higher API-traffic bandwidth on the external side.
  • Latency-sensitive networking between regions for global customer base.

For training clusters, the GPU clusters docs say nodes connect over 3.2 Tb/s InfiniBand. The inference cluster is different. The October 2026 IBM Cloud post says its B300 capacity uses NVIDIA Spectrum-X Ethernet, so Together now runs both fabrics, InfiniBand for training clusters and Ethernet for at least this inference build.

Scaling capacity

Together's capacity scales in step with traffic. As API traffic grows, more capacity comes online. The blended owned/partner model means scaling can happen at multiple speeds — partners absorb fast spikes; owned capacity provides the baseline.

✓ Check yourself

Answer from memory before opening each one.

  1. Why does owning GPUs help the economics of inference, and when is renting the better choice?

    Show answer

    Owned GPUs spread their fixed cost over many busy hours, and renting adds the lessor's margin. Renting is better when demand is spiky or uncertain, because owned GPUs would sit idle. See owned hardware and partner capacity.

  2. Steady load needs 100 GPUs, and for two hours a day it spikes to 160. What does the hybrid model do, and what would owning 160 cost?

    Show answer

    Own about 100 and rent the extra 60 for those two hours. Owning 160 would give 100 × 24 + 60 × 2 = 2,520 busy GPU-hours out of 3,840, about 66% utilisation, with 60 GPUs idle most of the day. See scaling capacity.

  3. Why do training and inference need different networks?

    Show answer

    Training synchronises across many GPUs every step, so inter-node bandwidth and latency set the pace. Inference mostly keeps a request inside one node, so the network concerns are external traffic and cross-region latency. See network.

  4. Customers cannot see which capacity serves a request. What does that opacity cost, and who carries the risk?

    Show answer

    Customers cannot audit where a request runs, which can matter for residency and compliance. Together must keep performance uniform across providers, and the weakest partner can define the experience. See partner capacity.

  5. Consumer cards cost less per hour. Why does a managed-inference platform still skip them?

    Show answer

    Cost per hour is not cost per token. Datacenter cards have more memory and bandwidth, so they serve more tokens per second and fit larger models, which lowers cost per token. See GPU mix.

Takeaway

  • Together mixes GPUs it owns in partner datacenters with capacity from neocloud partners, and customers see only "Together inference."
  • Owning GPUs gives better unit economics for high-utilisation inference, and it is one of the largest uses of the capital it has raised.
  • Owned capacity carries the base load and partner capacity absorbs spikes, so capacity can grow at two speeds.
  • The fleet is datacenter-grade (H100, H200, Blackwell and some A100) with no consumer cards.
  • Inference needs less inter-GPU bandwidth than training. The training clusters use InfiniBand, while the IBM Cloud B300 inference cluster uses Spectrum-X Ethernet.
