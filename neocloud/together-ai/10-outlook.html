Section D · Position

Outlook

Together is one of the most interesting bets in the AI infrastructure landscape because it sits at the intersection of open-source momentum and managed-inference economics. The strategic question is which forces — hyperscaler absorption, open-source continued growth, frontier-capability gap — dominate over the next several years.

Bull case

  1. Open-source maintains capability momentum. Llama, Qwen, DeepSeek, others continue narrowing the gap with closed-source frontier; Together's catalog stays competitive.
  2. Inference market grows faster than training. The shift toward inference (driven by AI product deployment) plays to Together's strength.
  3. Cost-sensitivity rises. As AI adoption scales, the per-token cost differential matters more to more customers, where one still exists: it is large against Opus- and Fable-class prices and gone against Haiku-class.
  4. Research advantage compounds. Together's optimization stack stays ahead of competitors; unit economics improve.
  5. Lifecycle bundling captures share. Customers value the inference + fine-tuning + training combo; Together captures lifetime value other platforms can't.

Bear case

  1. Hyperscalers absorb open-source. AWS / Azure / GCP make Together's value proposition redundant for customers already in their orbit.
  2. Open-source quality stalls. Frontier closed-source pulls further ahead; the open-source segment becomes a niche rather than a mainstream alternative.
  3. Margin compression. Inference becomes commoditized; per-token prices fall faster than cost-per-token; margins compress.
  4. Closed-source pricing pressure. This has started. Anthropic's Haiku 5.5 lists at $0.10 input and $0.50 output per million tokens, level with Together's cheapest tier, and Sonnet 5.5 at $2 and $10 is within 30 to 56% of GLM-5.3 (November 2026). Further cuts would leave only the structural reasons, fine-tuning and data control, for choosing open.
  5. Specialist competitors fragment market. Fireworks, Lepton, Anyscale, others each take pieces of the inference market.

Scenarios

Scenario A: The platform leader

Together becomes the dominant managed-inference platform for open-source models. Multi-billion-dollar annual revenue; eventually a public listing. Strategic value as the AI ecosystem's open-source counterpart to closed-source frontier.

Scenario B: Profitable specialist

Together carves out a sustainable position in the open-source inference market at moderate scale. Profitable software business; maintained as private or modest exit.

Scenario C: Squeezed by hyperscalers

Hyperscaler-hosted open-source absorbs the broader market; Together is left with the customers who specifically want the platform's research-credibility and lifecycle-platform features. Smaller but persistent.

Scenario D: Acquisition

A hyperscaler or AI company acquires Together for the research talent and platform technology. Premium price; consolidation move.

What to watch

  • Open-source model release cadence and capability progression.
  • Closed-source pricing moves (OpenAI / Anthropic / Google).
  • Hyperscaler open-source-inference offering evolution.
  • Together's revenue growth disclosures or reports.
  • Major customer wins or strategic partnerships.
  • IPO signals or other liquidity events.
  • Research output continuity.

✓ Check yourself

Answer from memory before opening each one.

  1. Several bear-case items share a root cause. Which ones, and what is it?

    Show answer

    Margin compression, closed-vendor price cuts and market fragmentation all come from commoditization, which pushes price down faster than cost. Hyperscaler absorption is a distribution problem, and a stalled open-model quality is a demand problem. See bear case.

  2. Open models cost $1 per unit of work and a closed vendor charges $4. The closed vendor halves its price. What happens to the saving, and what do you check?

    Show answer

    The saving falls from 75% to 50%. It is still positive, but smaller, so check whether it still covers migration, evaluation and any quality gap. See bear case.

  3. In the hyperscaler-squeeze scenario Together survives smaller. What does it keep that customers still value?

    Show answer

    Its research-backed serving, fast access to new models and lifecycle tooling, for customers who care about those more than convenience. See scenarios.

  4. How would you tell early that open-model quality is stalling rather than that closed models are pulling ahead?

    Show answer

    Track your own eval scores for the best open and closed models across several release cycles. A stall shows as the open score flattening while the closed score keeps rising. See what to watch.

Takeaway

  • The bull case needs open models to keep pace, inference to outgrow training, cost sensitivity to rise, the research edge to compound and lifecycle bundling to win share.
  • The bear case is hyperscalers absorbing open models, open quality stalling, commoditized margins, closed-vendor price cuts that have already reached the cheap end, and specialist rivals splitting the market.
  • Four outcomes are plausible: platform leader, profitable specialist, squeezed by hyperscalers, or acquired.
  • The watch list is open-model releases, closed-vendor price moves, hyperscaler offers, revenue disclosures, large customer wins, liquidity signals and research output.
  • Together is a clean test of whether open-source AI works as a commercial business.
