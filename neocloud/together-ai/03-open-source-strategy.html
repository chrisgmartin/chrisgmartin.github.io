Section B · Product

The Open-Source Strategy

Together's bet that curated, optimized open-source models could be a viable alternative to closed-source APIs has shaped the company's entire product strategy. The quality leg of the thesis has aged well as Llama, Qwen, DeepSeek and GLM matched closed models on many tasks. The cost leg has weakened since closed vendors added cheap tiers.

Why open-source models

Together's strategic bet was that customers would care about more than absolute frontier capability:

  • Cost. Per-token pricing on optimized open-source serving is often well below closed frontier APIs. Together's July 2026 Series C post claims companies routinely cut costs 6x to 20x by moving to open models.
  • Customization. Open-source models can be fine-tuned. Closed-source frontier models can't be fine-tuned at the level open-source allows.
  • Data control. Some customers don't want to send sensitive data through proprietary model APIs.
  • Regulatory. Some sectors require model traceability that open-source enables.
  • Optionality. Customers want to avoid lock-in to a single closed-source provider.

These motivations support a real market for open-source-hosted inference. Together's bet was that the market would be large enough to support a platform business.

The curation problem

Open-source model releases happen constantly. Llama family alone has multiple variants per release; Mistral, Qwen, DeepSeek release at their own cadence. Customers don't want to evaluate every model release.

Together's curation:

  • Maintain a catalog of the genuinely competitive open-source models.
  • Deprecate models that have been surpassed.
  • Publish benchmarks and quality comparisons.
  • Maintain instruction-tuned variants for chat-style use cases.
  • Surface the right model for the right use case.

This is a real product investment that customers value. Doing this well requires deep model evaluation expertise — exactly what Together's research lineage supports.

Performance gap

How does open-source quality compare to closed-source frontier?

  • 2022-early-2023: large gap. GPT-4 was meaningfully ahead of any open-source model.
  • Late 2023-2024: gap narrowed. Llama 2/3, Mixtral, etc. were competitive on many benchmarks.
  • 2025-2026: Together's Series C post says DeepSeek, Nemotron, MiniMax, Kimi and GLM have closed the quality gap with proprietary frontier models. That is the company's claim, not an independent benchmark.
  • 2024-2026: gap variable by task. For many tasks, open-source is close to or matches closed-source frontier. For some (reasoning-heavy, coding, agentic), closed-source retains a lead.

The narrowing gap is the strongest tailwind for Together's strategy. The remaining gap is the constraint.

Commercial advantages

From a customer's perspective, the per-token economics on open-source-hosted inference at Together vs closed-source frontier APIs:

  • Often cheaper per token, but the gap now depends on the pair (see the pricing chapter). As of November 2026, GLM-5.3 at $1.40 input and $4.40 output per million tokens is 30 to 56% below Claude Sonnet 5.5 at $2 and $10, while Kimi K3 at $3 and $15 costs more than Sonnet 5.5.
  • Slightly different latency profile (variable depending on model and serving optimization).
  • Can be much cheaper for high-volume workloads where the latency advantage of closed-source frontier isn't needed.

For workloads where open-source quality is "good enough" — many enterprise use cases — the cost savings make Together a strong economic choice.

Ecosystem positioning

Together has positioned itself as a friendly counterparty to the open-source ecosystem rather than a competitor:

  • Contributing back to open-source projects (FlashAttention, etc.).
  • Releasing models (RedPajama, etc.).
  • Publishing research that benefits the broader community.
  • Hosting models from many providers without playing favorites.

This posture builds goodwill that translates into customer trust. Compared to managed-inference competitors who are more closed about their stack, Together's research-and-open-source presence is differentiated.

Risks

  • Closed-source frontier reasserts dominance. If OpenAI / Anthropic / Google maintain enough quality lead, customers stick with closed APIs.
  • Open-source quality plateaus. Without continued investment from Meta and others, the catalog of competitive models could stagnate.
  • Hyperscalers absorb open-source. AWS Bedrock, Azure AI, GCP Vertex all offer open-source model hosting. Customers may default to their existing cloud rather than to Together.
  • Margin compression. As open-source-inference becomes commoditized, prices fall and Together's margins compress.

✓ Check yourself

Answer from memory before opening each one.

  1. Of the five reasons to choose open models, which depend on something closed models cannot offer, and which depend only on price?

    Show answer

    Fine-tuning access, data control and traceability are structural, because closed APIs limit or forbid them. Cost is only a price gap, which a closed vendor can narrow with a price cut. The cost advantage is therefore the weakest of the five. See why open-source models.

  2. Customers do not want to evaluate every new model release. What does Together do about that, and why is it hard to copy well?

    Show answer

    It curates the catalog, retires surpassed models, publishes comparisons and recommends models by use case. Doing this takes continuing evaluation expertise and the operations work to serve each model well, which is a recurring cost rather than a one-off. See the curation problem.

  3. A legal-tech firm must summarise confidential contracts at 200M tokens a day. Which factors favour open models on Together, and what do you test first?

    Show answer

    Volume favours the cost saving, and data-control needs favour a provider with clear retention terms. Test first whether an open model meets quality on the firm's own contracts, then read the data-handling terms. See commercial advantages.

  4. Benchmarks show open models closing the gap. Why might a given customer still stay with a closed API?

    Show answer

    Benchmarks average across tasks, while the customer's workload may sit in the reasoning, coding or agentic areas where closed models lead. Switching also costs evaluation and procurement effort. Judge the gap with your own eval set. See performance gap.

  5. Why does hyperscalers hosting open models threaten Together even when Together is cheaper?

    Show answer

    Customers already inside a hyperscaler have contracts, security reviews and consolidated billing. Once the same model is available there, the saving must beat the friction of adding a vendor. See risks.

Takeaway

  • Open models appeal for five reasons: lower cost, the ability to fine-tune, control over data, traceability for regulators, and freedom from a single closed provider.
  • Curation is a product: keep the genuinely competitive models, retire the surpassed ones, publish comparisons and match model to use case.
  • The quality gap to closed frontier models narrowed from 2022 to 2024 and now varies by task. Closed models keep a lead on reasoning-heavy, coding and agentic work.
  • Per-token prices on optimized open models are often below closed frontier APIs, but the gap varies by pair and the largest open models can cost more than mid-tier closed ones. It matters most for high-volume work that does not need frontier quality.
  • The risks are a renewed closed lead, a plateau in open models, hyperscalers hosting the same models, and margin compression.
